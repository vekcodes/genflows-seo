GenFlows — Owner SEO Plan

The plan at owner level: backlinks, confirming the website is genuinely fixed, measurement and ongoing research. This is the strategic wrapper around the two existing task lists — it says what we are doing, in what order, who owns it and what "done" looks like. The task-level detail already lives in GenFlows_Website_Tech_Spec_2026-07-27 (developer) and GenFlows_Marketing_Actions_2026-07-27 (marketing).

Date: 29 July 2026  ·  Property: www.genflows.com (HubSpot portal 49399880)  ·  Source audit: GenFlows_SEO_AEO_Report_2026-07-21

The situation in one paragraph. Content volume is no longer the problem — roughly 81 posts are live or drafted and on-page hygiene is clean. Three things hold rankings back, and none of them is a writing problem. (1) Most posts are too young for Google to have ranked them yet. (2) The domain has almost no backlinks, so it cannot outrank established competitors on commercial terms regardless of content quality. (3) A set of technical defects — sitemap gaps, duplicated head tags, the Tailwind Play CDN, stale /v3 and /v5 pages — means part of the library is not even being discovered cleanly. Backlinks and the technical fixes are the two levers that actually move the needle now. Everything else in this document exists to support those two.

W1 · Confirm the website is actually fixed P0

Owner: developer, plus whoever controls the HubSpot theme and DNS. This gates everything else.

Nothing compounds until this is closed. The specification is already written — this workstream is about verifying it shipped in production, not re-planning it. Do not mark it done on "we deployed"; mark it done on "the checks pass on the live site."

What has to be true

  1. Sitemap. Every published post present (18 were missing at audit), sitemap declared in robots.txt, no post frozen at 1970-01-01.
  2. Head / theme cleanup. Duplicated canonical, OG and title tags removed. The Tailwind Play CDN removed — it is a development-only script and a real performance drag. Leftover AMP removed.
  3. Stale pages resolved. /v3, /v5 and any other orphan or test URLs 301-redirected, not merely unlinked from navigation.
  4. Structured data. One consolidated JSON-LD graph per blog post, replacing the theme's two competing blocks. Organisation schema on the homepage. FAQPage schema on posts. llms.txt published.
  5. Verification pass. Run the one-command verification set in the tech spec appendix, then run it again seven days later to confirm nothing regressed on the next theme publish.
Definition of done — every check in the tech spec appendix passes on the live site, Search Console reports no coverage errors, and Core Web Vitals are green on mobile for a representative blog post URL.

W2 · Backlinks & digital PR P0

Owner: owner / founder, with team support. Target: 15–25 quality referring domains in the first 90 days.

We are targeting commercial head terms where incumbents have years of domain age and hundreds of referring domains. On-page quality does not beat that — links do. Relevance matters far more than volume: one link from a RevOps or GTM newsletter is worth more than fifty directory listings.

Tier 1 · Foundation — first two weeks, free

Not really link building, but it is the floor everyone else already has.

  • Claim and fully complete: G2, Clutch, Crunchbase, LinkedIn company page, and the relevant agency directories.
  • Founder and team profiles pointing at the site: LinkedIn, X, personal site, every podcast and speaker bio.
  • Tool-ecosystem listings — the most overlooked win. We already display Clay Artisan Solutions Partner, Smartlead Certified Partner and First 100 Partner badges. Ask each partner for a directory listing or partner-page link. Also: n8n community, HubSpot Solutions Directory. These are high-relevance and usually free.

Tier 2 · The engine — weeks 3 to 12

  • Podcast guesting — highest ROI for an agency. Target RevOps, B2B sales and GTM shows. Two per month. Every appearance gives a show-notes link plus distribution to a buying audience.
  • Original data / benchmark report. We already have a "B2B Outbound Benchmarks 2026" post. Turn it into a standalone annual report using real numbers from client campaigns. Data is the single most linkable asset type, because other people cite statistics.
  • Guest posts and expert contributions. Pitch RevOps and sales-tech publications. Two per month.
  • Expert-quote sourcing. Answer journalist requests in our lane — outbound, deliverability, GTM engineering. Low effort, occasionally lands a high-authority link.
  • Comparison-page outreach. When a "best cold email tools" or "top GTM agencies" roundup exists and we are absent, pitch inclusion.

Tier 3 · Partnerships — ongoing

  • Co-marketing with complementary, non-competing vendors and agencies — a data provider, a deliverability tool, a CRM consultancy. Joint webinar or joint guide, mutual links.
  • Client case studies published on partner sites, linking back.
Do not: buy link packages, use private blog networks, or mass-blast guest-post offers. On a young domain that is a live risk to the whole property, not a theoretical one.

How we track it

The sheet One tracker: target, contact, channel, status, date pitched, live URL, referring domain. Reviewed weekly.
The scoreboard Referring-domain count, checked monthly. That single number is how we judge whether this workstream is happening.
Pipeline rule Keep 30+ live prospects in the sheet at all times, or the weekly slot runs dry and quietly stops.

W3 · Measurement P0

Owner: owner / marketing. Small effort, and it unblocks every query-level decision.

What to put in place

  • Google Search Console verified for the domain and the sitemap submitted — then 28 days of impression data collected before drawing any conclusions. A large part of the audit's guidance is explicitly blocked on this.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools as well — it feeds ChatGPT's search, so it matters for AI-search visibility.
  • GA4 confirmed firing on all blog pages, with the de-duplication date noted so historical numbers are read correctly.
  • Backlink monitoring: one tool only — Ahrefs, Semrush, or the free Search Console links report — checked monthly.
Monthly one-pager — referring domains, indexed pages, total impressions, top 10 queries, clicks, and demo requests attributed to organic. One page, same six numbers every month, so the trend is visible at a glance.

W4 · Research P1

Owner: marketing, with the automation doing the heavy pulls. Feeds W2 and W5.

Four recurring research jobs

  1. Keyword and gap research — quarterly. Pull competitors' ranking keywords, find where they rank and we have nothing, and add those to the planned backlog in blog-tracker.md. Prioritise commercial intent ("X agency", "best X software", "X vs Y") over purely informational terms, because those are what convert.
  2. AEO / AI-search research — monthly. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode the 20 questions our buyers actually ask ("best GTM engineering agency", "how to fix cold email deliverability"). Record whether GenFlows is cited. That citation rate is the AEO metric; FAQ schema and clear question-format headings are how we improve it.
  3. Competitor watch — quarterly. What are the top five competitors publishing, and where are they getting links from? Their backlink profile is our prospect list for W2.
  4. Link-prospect research — continuous. Podcasts, publications, directories and roundups worth pitching. This is what keeps the 30+ prospect pipeline full.

W5 · Content P1

Owner: the existing batch process — no change to the mechanics, a change to the mix.

What changes

  • Keep the batch cadence and keep using blog-tracker.md as the single source of truth to prevent duplicates.
  • Shift from volume to depth. With 80+ posts, the next win is upgrading the ten posts closest to ranking — positions 11 to 30 in Search Console — rather than publishing ten new ones. Refresh, expand, add FAQ schema, add internal links.
  • Build two or three linkable assets: the benchmark report, a free ICP or template tool, a calculator. These exist to attract links rather than traffic — they are the bridge between W5 and W2.
  • Internal linking discipline: every new post links out to three or more existing posts, and gets linked from at least two.

What to realistically expect

PeriodWhat happens
Months 1–2Almost no ranking movement. Indexing coverage and impressions improve. This is normal and is not evidence the plan is failing — it is the ranking-latency window described in the audit.
Months 3–4Long-tail terms begin ranking. First organic demo requests arrive.
Months 6+Competitive commercial terms become reachable — only if the backlink work was actually done consistently across the whole period.
The single biggest risk. Treating backlinks as a project instead of a habit. Content is already systematised and runs itself; link acquisition is not, and it is the part that decides the outcome. Whoever owns W2 needs a protected recurring weekly slot — otherwise it will quietly not happen, and in six months the honest diagnosis will be that the plan was never executed rather than that it did not work.

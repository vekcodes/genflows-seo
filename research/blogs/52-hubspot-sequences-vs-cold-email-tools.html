TL;DR

  • Sequence emails leave from your own mailbox, not HubSpot's servers. HubSpot documents this explicitly. So every deliverability consequence of cold volume lands on your primary domain and your own Google or Microsoft tenant.
  • The real ceiling is not 500 emails a day. That cap counts every send in the sequence, including follow-ups scheduled from earlier enrollments — and bulk enrollment is capped at 50 contacts at a time, throttled to three emails a minute.
  • HubSpot documents no warmup and no mailbox rotation. Cold email tools are built on the opposite architecture: many mailboxes across many secondary domains, each throttled to roughly 20–30 sends a day.
  • HubSpot's Anti-Spam Policy requires express, verifiable, documented opt-in for messages sent through its services. That is a consent standard most cold prospecting lists do not meet.
  • The right answer is usually both. The cold tool sends and absorbs the risk; HubSpot owns the record, the deal, and every warm follow-up after the reply.

You already pay for HubSpot. Sequences are sitting right there in the Sales Hub nav, they do multi-step email with delays and auto-unenroll on reply, and buying a second tool to send email feels like paying twice for the same thing. It is a completely reasonable question, and plenty of teams try it before they buy a dedicated sender.

The answer is not "HubSpot is bad at email." It is that Sequences and cold email tools are built on two different architectures, for two different jobs, and the mismatch shows up in your domain reputation rather than in the feature list. This post covers exactly what HubSpot documents about how Sequences send, where the real limits bite, what the cold tools do structurally differently, and the split that works in practice.

The Short Answer

Use HubSpot Sequences for warm, low-volume, one-to-one email where the contact already knows you: inbound follow-up, existing-customer expansion, event follow-up, and a hand-built list of named accounts. Use a dedicated cold email tool such as Smartlead or Instantly for cold volume, because it spreads sending across many mailboxes on secondary domains with warmup and rotation, keeping the reputation risk off the domain your contracts and invoices run on. If you are doing both — and most outbound teams are — the clean setup is the cold tool sends, then pushes replies into HubSpot so the CRM owns the record from the reply onward.

The Fact That Decides This: Where Sequence Emails Actually Come From

This is the part most comparisons get backwards, so it is worth being precise. HubSpot documents two entirely separate sending paths:

  • Marketing email goes out "through HubSpot's own email sending network," on HubSpot's shared IP addresses by default, using your configured email sending domain (VERIFIED).
  • Sequences, one-to-one emails, and the conversations inbox "do not use HubSpot's email sending network" — they are "sent over the network of the inbox you connected" (VERIFIED).

So a sequence email is not a HubSpot email in any infrastructure sense. It is a Gmail or Outlook message that HubSpot scheduled on your behalf. Your mailbox sends it, your domain signs it, and your Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 tenant carries the reputation.

The common misconception to discard: that cold emailing through Sequences puts "HubSpot's shared IPs" at risk. It does not — that is marketing email. The risk is narrower and more personal: it is your primary domain, with no rotation and no warmup to absorb the damage (directional inference from the two documented facts above).

That distinction matters because provider thresholds are enforced per domain. Google requires senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail to authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC and to keep spam rates in Postmaster Tools below 0.30%, with 0.10% as the target it actually wants (VERIFIED). Yahoo requires the same authentication trio and a spam rate below 0.3% (VERIFIED). Microsoft rejects non-compliant high-volume mail outright with 550 5.7.515 Access denied, sending domain does not meet the required authentication level, enforced since 2025 (VERIFIED requirement; exact rollout date directional). A 0.30% spam rate is roughly three complaints per thousand delivered messages. Cold lists do not reliably stay under that — and when the sender is your primary domain, a bad campaign degrades deliverability for your invoices, contracts, support replies, and warm follow-up at the same time.

The Limits That Bite First (And They Are Not the Daily Cap)

HubSpot publishes its sequence limits clearly, and they are more restrictive than the headline number suggests.

Limit What HubSpot documents Why it matters for outbound
Daily sends 500/day on a Professional seat, 1,000/day on Enterprise, on a rolling 24-hour window Rolling, not a midnight reset — you cannot batch around it
What counts Sends, not enrollments — including scheduled follow-ups from earlier enrollments and paused ones The big one. On a 4-step sequence at steady state, new-prospect capacity is a fraction of 500
Bulk enrollment 50 contacts at a time, rate-limited to a maximum of three emails per minute Loading a 2,000-contact list becomes 40 manual batches
Shared mailbox If several users share one connected inbox, the cap applies to the mailbox in total You cannot add seats to raise throughput on one mailbox
One sequence per contact A contact can only be enrolled in one sequence at a time No parallel plays; sensible for warm outreach, awkward for testing
Templates per sequence Maximum 10 email templates (task reminders unlimited) Rarely a constraint — most cold sequences are 3–5 steps
Tier required Sales Hub or Service Hub Professional or Enterprise, with an assigned seat Not available on Starter or Free at all
Warmup / rotation Not documented anywhere in the Sequences knowledge base The two primitives cold sending depends on are simply absent

Read the second row again, because it is the correction that changes the maths. "500 emails per day" is not "500 prospects per day." If your sequence has four email steps, then at steady state every new prospect you enroll today also generates three future sends that count against the same rolling cap. Your true new-contact throughput is closer to 100–125 a day than 500 (directional — depends entirely on your step count and delays).

What the Cold Tools Do Structurally Differently

Smartlead and Instantly are not "Sequences with more features." They are built around a different unit: the mailbox pool.

  • Unlimited email accounts on every plan. Both vendors document this, including on their entry tiers (VERIFIED). HubSpot's model is one connected inbox per user, and more capacity means more seats.
  • Per-mailbox throttling. Instantly documents a recommended 30 campaign emails per account per day, with its done-for-you mailboxes capped at 20, and a campaign limit formula of accounts × 30 (VERIFIED — note the 30 is guidance, not an enforced ceiling).
  • Mailbox rotation. Sends are distributed across the pool so no single account exceeds a safe threshold (VERIFIED for Smartlead; Instantly's rotation is documented at blog and help-centre level — directional).
  • Built-in warmup. Unlimited warmup on all plans at both vendors, with warmup traffic excluded from campaign send limits (VERIFIED).
  • Spintax to vary copy across sends so identical bodies do not build a spam fingerprint (VERIFIED, both).
  • A unified reply inbox across every connected sender, so a 40-mailbox setup does not mean 40 inboxes to check (VERIFIED, both).

On price, as of July 2026: Smartlead starts at $39/month (2,000 contacts, 6,000 sends) with its Pro tier at $94; Instantly's outreach-only plans start at $47/month with bundle plans from $94 (VERIFIED on both vendor pricing pages — check current pricing before quoting it). Compare that to Sales Hub Professional at roughly $90 per seat per month plus a one-time onboarding fee (directional — sourced from HubSpot's own blog rather than its pricing page). The Consent Question, Quoted Accurately

It is worth being careful here, because plenty of content on this topic overstates it. HubSpot does not say "cold email is banned." What its Anti-Spam Policy says (last modified February 2026) is that you are "strictly prohibited from using the HubSpot Services to directly contact recipients from purchased, rented, borrowed, or other third-party lists… in violation of applicable laws or third-party terms" — that qualifier is in the original. Separately, it requires that messages sent through HubSpot Services go only to recipients who have "provided express, verifiable consent," "opted in through a clear, documented process," and not withdrawn it (VERIFIED, both quotes).

Read plainly: a scraped or enriched prospecting list does not clear an "express, verifiable, documented opt-in" bar, whatever the local legality of the send. Note also that the Sequences documentation itself says nothing about cold or purchased lists either way — the governing language lives only in the legal centre. Draw your own conclusion, but draw it from the actual text. Our cold email compliance guide covers GDPR and CAN-SPAM separately, and none of this is legal advice.

When HubSpot Sequences Are Genuinely the Right Tool

This is not a one-sided comparison. There are jobs where Sequences beat any cold email tool, and one thing they can do that neither marketing email nor a cold sender can:

  • Warm inbound follow-up. Sending from the rep's real mailbox on the real company domain is precisely what you want when the prospect asked to be contacted. Sequences auto-unenroll on reply or booked meeting (VERIFIED).
  • Existing customers and expansion. Documented consent already exists, which satisfies the policy standard above.
  • Contacts who opted out of marketing email. HubSpot documents that these contacts "can still be sent one-to-one emails" (VERIFIED). That is a real capability marketing email does not have — and a genuine reason to keep Sequences in the stack.
  • Small, hand-built named-account lists. The 50-contact bulk cap is painful at volume and irrelevant for 25 target accounts.
  • Task-based multichannel plays. Unlimited task reminders alongside the 10-template cap make Sequences a decent call-plus-LinkedIn-plus-email orchestrator rather than an email cannon (VERIFIED).

The Setup That Actually Works: Both, With Clear Boundaries

Almost every outbound team we work with lands in the same place. The division of labour looks like this:

  1. Cold sending lives outside HubSpot, on secondary domains and a warmed mailbox pool, in Smartlead or Instantly. Your primary domain never touches a cold list.
  2. HubSpot becomes the record of truth at the reply. Both tools ship native HubSpot integrations. Smartlead's is the more explicitly documented of the two: it syncs leads, auto-creates deals at a stage you choose, and logs activity — opens, clicks, unsubscribes, and bounces as Notes; sends and replies as Email activities — with triggers including Replied, Clicked, and Identified Interest (VERIFIED). Its documented limitation: it creates deals at the specified stage but does not move them between stages.
  3. Instantly's integration is real but less specified. It splits across two connectors — one for exporting into workflows, one for importing HubSpot leads — and does not document field mappings, sync frequency, or capacity limits (VERIFIED absence). Do not assume the two integrations are equivalent.
  4. Sequences take over after the handoff. Positive reply, no-show recovery, post-demo nurture, expansion — all from the rep's real mailbox, all logged natively.
  5. Suppression flows back. Anyone unsubscribed, closed-lost, or already in a live deal must be blocked in the cold tool. This is the step teams skip, and it is how customers receive cold pitches.

Two adjacent pieces make this setup work properly: getting the data in cleanly (see Clay + HubSpot sync) and getting replies to a human fast (see lead routing and speed-to-lead). It is also worth defining what a replied lead counts as in your funnel, which we cover in MQL vs SQL vs SAL.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use HubSpot Sequences for cold email?

Technically the feature will send, but HubSpot's Anti-Spam Policy requires that messages sent through its services go only to recipients who gave express, verifiable consent and opted in through a clear, documented process, and it prohibits contacting recipients from purchased, rented, or third-party lists in violation of applicable laws or third-party terms. A scraped or enriched prospecting list does not meet that standard. The practical risk is separate and just as real: sequence emails send from your own mailbox on your primary domain.

How many emails can HubSpot Sequences send per day?

Up to 500 a day on a Sales or Service Professional seat and 1,000 on Enterprise, measured on a rolling 24-hour window rather than resetting at midnight. Critically, the cap counts every send in the sequence including follow-ups scheduled from earlier enrollments, so your actual new-prospect capacity is a fraction of that number. If multiple users share one connected inbox, the limit applies to the mailbox in total.

Do HubSpot sequence emails send from HubSpot's servers or my own mailbox?

Your own. HubSpot documents that sequences, one-to-one emails, and the conversations inbox do not use HubSpot's email sending network and are sent over the network of the inbox you connected. Only marketing email uses HubSpot's network and its shared IP addresses. That means the deliverability consequences of a cold campaign land on your domain and your mail tenant, not on HubSpot's infrastructure.

Why not just add more mailboxes to HubSpot instead of buying Smartlead?

Sequences are tied to one connected personal inbox per user, and HubSpot documents no mailbox rotation and no warmup — the two primitives cold sending depends on. Adding capacity means buying more Sales Hub seats at roughly $90 each per month. Smartlead and Instantly both include unlimited email accounts on every plan, starting at $39 and $47 a month respectively as of July 2026.

Will cold emailing from my company domain hurt deliverability?

It can, and the thresholds are per domain. Google requires senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail to authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC and keep Postmaster-reported spam rates below 0.30%, with 0.10% as its stated target. Yahoo requires the same authentication and a spam rate below 0.3%. Microsoft rejects non-compliant high-volume mail with a 550 5.7.515 error. Roughly three complaints per thousand messages is all the headroom you get, and a damaged primary domain also degrades your invoices, contracts, and support email.

Do Smartlead and Instantly integrate with HubSpot?

Both have native integrations, but they are not equivalent. Smartlead's documents syncing leads, auto-creating deals at a chosen stage, and logging activity — opens, clicks, unsubscribes, and bounces as Notes, sends and replies as Email activities — with triggers including Replied and Clicked; it does not move deals between stages. Instantly offers two connectors, one for exporting and one for importing HubSpot leads, but does not document field mappings, sync frequency, or limits.

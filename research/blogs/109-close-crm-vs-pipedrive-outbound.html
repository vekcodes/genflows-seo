  • Close has a native dialer; Pipedrive does not.   • Both have sequences, but they enrol different things. Close Workflows run email, SMS and task steps against leads and contacts. Pipedrive Sequences are email-and-task flows into which you enrol deals.
  • Pipedrive’s sticker price looks lower, but we cannot print it. Pipedrive’s pricing page blocked our automated check, so this post gives you Close’s verified numbers and tells you exactly what to confirm on Pipedrive’s live page instead of quoting stale third-party figures.
  • Neither sends LinkedIn messages or replaces a cold email sender. At volume you still run Smartlead or Instantly for sending and HeyReach for LinkedIn, with the CRM as the system of record.
  • The rule of thumb: phone-and-email prospecting teams pick Close; teams running a visual, longer-cycle deal pipeline with lighter outbound pick Pipedrive.

When SDRs are working 200 accounts a week across cold email, LinkedIn and phone, which CRM holds that activity in a shape you can actually run and report on?

The Short Answer

Choose Close if outbound is your core motion and the phone is part of it. You get a native power dialer, native SMS, email and task automation and a lead-centric record where a company and its contacts live together. The trade-off is that the useful outbound features (Workflows, power dialer, bulk email) start at the Growth tier, not the cheapest one.

Choose Pipedrive if your revenue motion is a visual deal pipeline with longer, relationship-led cycles and outbound is one input among several. Expect to assemble calling and SMS from Marketplace apps, and to treat deals, not leads, as the unit that moves through sequences.

If you send cold email at scale, neither CRM is your sender. See the cold-email section below.

The Data Model: Lead-Centric vs Deal-Centric

In Close, a Lead is usually a company, with one or more Contacts under it and Opportunities attached to the lead. Smart Views can be built on leads or on contacts. That structure matches how outbound actually works: you research an account, add three or four stakeholders, contact them across channels, and only later create an opportunity if someone engages. (See our guide to Close Smart Views for outbound.)

Pipedrive organises work around Deals, with Persons and Organizations as supporting records and a separate Leads inbox as a pre-deal holding area (its API exposes deals, persons, organizations and leads as separate objects). That model is excellent when each opportunity is a card moving across a pipeline. It is less natural when you have 4,000 contacted prospects and 60 live opportunities, because most of your activity has no deal to hang on. (Directional: this is our assessment of fit, not a vendor claim.)

Why this matters in practice: in Close, “who at this account did we contact, on which channel, and what happened?” is answered on one lead page. In a deal-centric CRM you either create a deal for every prospect (which pollutes pipeline reporting) or keep prospect activity in the leads inbox and reconcile later.

Email and Sequences

Close runs automation through Workflows (Growth and above): multi-step cadences that mix email, SMS and tasks, with bulk email and configurable sending limits on the same tiers. A contact can only receive a given sequence once.

Pipedrive has Sequences, part of its Pulse prospecting suite: a drag-and-drop canvas of emails and activities with delays (including skipping weekends), enrolment one at a time or in bulk, and progress tracking. The important detail from Pipedrive’s own product page is that you enrol deals into sequences. Separately, Campaigns by Pipedrive is a paid add-on aimed at email marketing to subscriber lists, not 1:1 outbound cadences.

Plan gating to verify: we could not confirm from Pipedrive’s pricing page which tier includes Sequences, and Close’s pricing page does not list “Sequences” as a separate line item. Check both live pricing pages for the exact tier before you commit.

Automation, Views and Reporting

  • Saved views: Close Smart Views are saved, filterable lists of leads or contacts that also drive bulk email and call queues. Pipedrive relies on filters and its pipeline views.
  • Automation: both have workflow automation; in Close it starts on Growth, in Pipedrive on a mid-tier plan (directional).
  • Reporting: Close adds workflow and email-template performance on Growth and Explorer-style custom graphs on Scale. Pipedrive’s strength is deal and pipeline reporting. Neither natively reports on LinkedIn activity, so you need the integration work below either way.
  • AI: Close includes monthly AI credits per user and an AI caller/assistant called Chloe, with AI email rewrite on Growth and above. Pipedrive’s AI Sales Assistant surfaces stalled deals and next best actions. Both are evolving fast; judge them in a trial, not on a feature page.

Cold Email, LinkedIn and Integrations

Whichever CRM you pick, a serious outbound programme sends cold email from dedicated infrastructure (Smartlead or Instantly on warmed secondary domains) and LinkedIn from a tool like HeyReach. Sending 2,000 cold emails a day from a CRM mailbox is how primary domains get burned. The CRM’s job is to be the record of who was contacted, what they replied and what happens next.

  • Close: open API on every plan. We have documented how to connect cold email tools to Close and HeyReach to Close; expect a middleware layer (Zapier, Make, Relay or n8n) rather than a native connector for several tools (directional).
  • Pipedrive: a large Marketplace and a documented API. We did not verify native connectors for Smartlead, Instantly or HeyReach; check each vendor’s CRM integration list before assuming.
If your sending stack is already wired to HubSpot, that may outweigh both options. Our Smartlead and HubSpot integration guide shows what native sync looks like.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Close or Pipedrive better for outbound sales?

For phone-and-email prospecting, Close is usually the better fit because calling, SMS, workflows and a lead-centric record are built in. Pipedrive suits a deal-pipeline-led motion with lighter outbound volume and fits best when you are comfortable adding calling apps.

Does Pipedrive have a built-in dialer?

No. Pipedrive’s help center says it does not have a built-in calling integration and directs users to Marketplace calling apps such as Aircall, Kixie, JustCall and CloudTalk. Its mobile apps can place and log calls.

Does Close have a native power dialer?

Yes. Calling and SMS are native on every Close plan, the power dialer is available from the Growth plan and the predictive dialer on Scale. Telephony usage is billed separately at pass-through rates.

Can Pipedrive run outbound email sequences?

Yes. Pipedrive Sequences let you build email and activity flows with delays and enrol deals individually or in bulk. The Campaigns add-on is a separate email-marketing tool. Confirm which plan includes Sequences on Pipedrive’s live pricing page.

Should I send cold email from my CRM?

Not at volume. Use a dedicated sender such as Smartlead or Instantly on separate, warmed domains, and sync replies and activity back into the CRM. Using CRM mailboxes for high-volume cold sending puts your primary domain’s reputation at risk.

What does lead-centric mean for outbound teams?

In Close a lead is typically a company with its contacts beneath it, so you can track outreach before any deal exists. In a deal-centric CRM, activity tends to attach to deals or people, which makes account-level prospecting harder to see and report on.

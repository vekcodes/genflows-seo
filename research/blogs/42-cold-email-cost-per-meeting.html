"How much does cold email cost?" has a dishonest answer and an honest one. The dishonest answer is the sticker price of a sending tool — $40 a month, cheap, done. The honest answer is a stack: domains, mailboxes, warmup, software, data, verification, and the human hours to run all of it. Most articles stop at the tool price because that's the number vendors want you anchored to. This one builds the whole model from the bottom up and terminates in the only figure that actually matters to a budget owner: your fully-loaded cost per meeting booked.

Prices below were gathered in mid-2026 from vendor pages and recent comparisons. Software tiers move constantly and much of this is secondary-sourced, so treat exact figures as "roughly, as of mid-2026" and spot-check the two or three you build your plan around.

The Short Answer

For a real sending operation — enough infrastructure to send a few thousand emails a week without torching deliverability — expect a tools-only cost of about $300–$700/month. Attach one human running it full-time and the true monthly cost becomes $7,000–$10,000+, because salary dwarfs software. An agency sits in between on cash-out ($3,000–$7,000/mo typical) but bundles the labor you'd otherwise hire. Translate any of those into results and you're usually looking at a cost per meeting somewhere between $40 and $800 depending on who's doing the work and how good the targeting and copy are.

Sending domains & mailboxes: the math that sets your floor

Your infrastructure size is dictated by one number: the ~40-emails-per-mailbox-per-day safe ceiling. Want to send 500 emails a day? That's roughly 500 ÷ 40 ≈ 13 mailboxes, plus a ~20% rotation buffer, so call it 16. At ~2–3 mailboxes per domain, that's 6–8 domains. Agency-scale operations running tens of thousands of sends a month commonly sit at 50–150 inboxes across 25–75 domains. This is exactly the sizing question we cover in the cold email infrastructure guide — and every one of those inboxes needs its own SPF, DKIM and DMARC records.

Reseller mailboxes are cheap for a reason. Panel/reseller mailboxes at ~$2.50/mo look like an easy 60% saving over native Google Workspace — but they can carry deliverability tradeoffs (shared infrastructure history, tenant reputation). For a low-stakes second channel they're fine; for your primary program, native Google/Microsoft mailboxes are the safer spend. (Reseller pricing is often an annual-locked loss-leader — directional.)

Warmup, software, data, verification

Warmup either costs you time (6–10 weeks per mailbox) or money (buy pre-warmed inboxes and skip the wait) — the tradeoff is covered in the warmup tools comparison. Sending software is genuinely cheap and rarely the thing to optimize; pick on features and deliverability, which the software comparison breaks down. Data is where budgets balloon — Clay's pricing alone can run $185–$495/mo. And verification is the one line nobody should cut: at $0.30–$8 per 1,000 it's the cheapest deliverability insurance you can buy, and the verification tools guide shows the ~10× price spread.

The Line Item Everyone Forgets: Human Labor

Here's the part DIY calculators quietly omit. Someone has to build the list, write the copy, load the campaign, monitor deliverability, and — critically — handle the replies. That's a job, not a background task.

  • Fractional / piecemeal: a freelance copywriter, list-builder, or campaign manager — variable, but real hours at real rates.
  • Full-time SDR: in the US, roughly $55k–$60k base and ~$83k–$85k OTE once commission is included, split about 70–80% base / 20–30% variable — plus tools, management overhead, and ramp time. (US-average band; varies widely by geography and seniority — directional.)

The takeaway: the moment a human is attached full-time, salary is 90%+ of your true cost and the software line becomes a rounding error. Any "cold email costs $50/month" claim is only counting the part that doesn't matter.

A Bottoms-Up Model: From Sends to Cost Per Meeting

Now assemble it. Cold email converts through a chain, and each stage has a rough 2026 benchmark (see the outbound benchmarks for the full picture):

StageTypical rateRunning total (from 10,000 sends)
Emails sent—10,000
Reply rate~2–5%~300 replies (at 3%)
Positive replies~10–20% of replies~45 positive (at 15%)
Booked meetings~25–40% of positives~15 meetings (at 33%)
Held (show-up)~65–80%~10–12 held meetings

That chain implies roughly one booked meeting per ~370–400 sends at median rates. Now divide your monthly spend by monthly meetings:

Worked example — DIY, tools-only: ~$600/mo stack, 10,000 sends/mo, ~15 booked meetings → ~$40/meeting on tools alone.
Same program, labor-loaded: add one SDR at ~$7,000/mo fully-loaded → ~$7,600/mo ÷ 15 meetings → ~$500/meeting.

The gap between those two numbers is the labor line. Any cost-per-meeting figure you see quoted below ~$40 is almost certainly a tool-only floor from a vendor that's ignoring the human running the machine — treat those as marketing, not budgeting.

In-House vs. Agency: The Break-Even Math

This is the decision the whole article is really about, and it's arithmetic, not preference. Three cost structures:

ModelMonthly costWhat you're buying
In-house (1 SDR + stack)~$7,000–$8,000/moFull control, owned process, but you carry hiring, ramp, and turnover risk.
Agency retainer~$3,000–$7,000/moBundled infra + labor + expertise. Note: the retainer is often only ~60–70% of true cost — some agencies bill domains/inboxes/data on top.
Pay-per-meeting~$500–$1,000 / booked meetingCost scales with results; ~$833 per held meeting at a 60% show rate. Risk shifts to the agency.

The crossover rule of thumb: if you can keep an SDR consistently productive and you'll run outbound for 12+ months, in-house usually wins on unit cost. If you need results in weeks not quarters, can't absorb ramp/turnover risk, or want variable cost tied to output, an agency or performance model wins. How to Cut Cost Per Meeting Without Wrecking Deliverability

The instinct is to send more. The lever that actually moves cost per meeting is reply rate, because it's the denominator in every stage below it. Doubling reply rate roughly halves cost per meeting at the same spend — and it's cheaper than doubling volume, which means more mailboxes, more warmup, and more deliverability risk. In order of impact:

  • List quality first. A tight verified, well-built list beats a big one — bad data burns domains and inflates cost per meeting on both ends.
  • Offer and targeting beat clever copy. The right message to the wrong list still fails.
  • Protect deliverability so the emails you already paid to send actually land — otherwise every other dollar is wasted upstream.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cold email cost per month in 2026?

A tools-only DIY stack — domains, mailboxes, warmup, software, data, and verification — runs roughly $300–$700/month for a serious sending operation, before any human time. Attach a full-time SDR and true cost rises to ~$7,000–$10,000/month. An agency retainer typically runs $3,000–$7,000/month with labor bundled in.

What is the average cost per meeting booked from cold email?

Roughly $40–$150 for a labor-light DIY program, $150–$400 for a well-run in-house program, and $300–$800 through an agency or pay-per-meeting model. Where you land depends far more on reply rate and list quality than on which tools you buy.

How much does a cold email agency cost?

Retainers typically run $2,500–$12,000/month, with $3,000–$7,000 common for done-for-you B2B. Pay-per-meeting models charge roughly $500–$1,000 per booked meeting. Watch for agencies that bill infrastructure and data on top of the retainer — the headline number is often only 60–70% of true cost.

Is it cheaper to hire an in-house SDR or use a cold email agency?

It's a break-even calculation. A US SDR costs ~$83k–$85k all-in (OTE); an agency retainer is ~$36k–$84k/year. If you can keep an SDR consistently productive over 12+ months, in-house usually wins on unit cost. If you need speed, want variable cost, or can't carry hiring and turnover risk, an agency or performance model wins.

How many domains and mailboxes do I need for cold email?

Work backward from the ~40-emails-per-mailbox-per-day safe cap. For 500 sends/day you need ~13 mailboxes plus a rotation buffer (~16), across ~6–8 domains. Agency-scale programs commonly run 50–150 inboxes across 25–75 domains.

What's the cheapest way to set up cold email infrastructure in 2026?

The budget stack is Cloudflare/Porkbun domains (~$10/yr), reseller mailboxes (~$2.50/mo), MillionVerifier for verification (~$0.55/1k at volume), and an entry SEP tier like Instantly (~$37/mo). But "cheapest" trades against deliverability and your time — reseller mailboxes and self-run warmup carry real risk, so cut infrastructure corners cautiously and never cut verification.

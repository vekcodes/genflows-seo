TL;DR

  • HubSpot's Quotes and CPQ tools moved out of Sales Hub in 2026. They now live in a standalone product, Revenue Hub, on the Professional or Enterprise tier — the old "it's part of Sales Hub Starter/Pro" mental model is out of date.
  • Two different approval systems get conflated constantly. Deal-stage approvals (blocking a deal from moving pipeline stages) require Sales Hub Enterprise. Quote and discount approvals (blocking a quote from going out) live in Revenue Hub. Most outbound teams only need the second one.
  • Native HubSpot Quotes is enough for most outbound-sourced deals. Standardized pricing, one-to-three package tiers, and lower-to-mid ACV — the typical shape of an SMB/mid-market outbound pipeline — don't need a dedicated CPQ platform.
  • Real CPQ (DealHub, PandaDoc, and similar) earns its cost when deals get complicated — usage-based pricing, multi-department approval chains, or subscription amendments and co-terming that native Quotes doesn't handle well.
  • E-signature is native and built into the quote flow, but HubSpot itself tells you to check legal validity with your own counsel — it isn't a blanket guarantee across every jurisdiction and use case.

An outbound rep gets a "yes" on a call — the prospect that came in through cold email or LinkedIn outreach is ready to buy. What happens in the next 20 minutes decides whether that deal closes this week or drags into a third follow-up call. That gap — verbal agreement to signed quote — is the part of the outbound-to-close pipeline almost nobody writes about, because most CPQ content is aimed at enterprise deal desks with multi-year contracts and legal review chains. Outbound-sourced deals are a different animal: higher volume, more standardized pricing, and a prospect whose attention span for a slow quote process is short.

The Short Answer

  • Quotes and CPQ now require Revenue Hub, not Sales Hub. Revenue Hub Professional lists at $95/seat/month ($57/seat/month bundled with an annual Sales Hub plan); Enterprise lists at $140/seat/month ($98/seat/month bundled).
  • Don't confuse deal-stage approvals with quote approvals. If all you need is "stop a rep from sending a quote with a 40% discount without sign-off," that's a Revenue Hub feature — you don't need Sales Hub Enterprise for it.
  • Most outbound pipelines are the exact profile native Quotes is built for: repeatable pricing, small package tiers, fast turnaround matters more than configurator depth.
  • The signal to look at real CPQ instead: usage-based billing, subscription proration/co-terming, or approval chains crossing more than one department.

What Changed in 2026: Quotes and CPQ Moved to Revenue Hub

HubSpot's own product page states it plainly: quote software "is available with Professional and Enterprise editions of Revenue Hub," a standalone product that absorbed and expanded what used to be a lighter Sales Hub feature and the earlier Commerce Hub. A Revenue Hub seat is required per user who creates or edits quotes, on top of the account-level subscription. If a piece of content you've read elsewhere says Quotes ships with Sales Hub Starter or Professional, treat it as stale — that was true under an older packaging structure and isn't the current gating.

What Revenue Hub Actually Includes

A Revenue Hub subscription covers the full quote-to-cash surface: branded quote templates pulling line items from the Product Library, native e-signature (buyer plus internal countersigner), one-time and recurring payment collection tied directly to the quote, and quote/discount approval rules. Base Product Library functionality — naming products, setting a price, adding a SKU — is available on every HubSpot plan, including the free CRM. What Revenue Hub adds on top is tiered and stair-step pricing, price books for regional variation, and the approval logic that reads those prices.

The Two Approval Systems Outbound Teams Confuse

This is the single most common mix-up in how HubSpot's deal-desk features get described, and it matters for what you actually need to buy.

Deal-Stage Approvals: Sales Hub Enterprise

This is a general pipeline gate — a Super Admin sets an approval stage on a pipeline, designates up to ten approvers, chooses "any one approver" or "all approvers" logic, and a deal simply cannot move past that stage without sign-off. It's a good fit if you want to enforce something like "no deal reaches Closed Won without a VP looking at it," independent of pricing. It requires Sales Hub Enterprise. We cover the pipeline side of this in more depth in HubSpot deal stages for outbound teams.

Quote and Discount Approvals: Revenue Hub

This is a narrower, pricing-specific gate: a rule tied to discount percentage, deal amount, or contract term that requires sign-off before a quote can be published to the prospect. Standard approvals are available on Revenue Hub Professional. Logic-based "advanced approvals" — rules that route automatically based on the numbers on the quote, with hard blocks on publishing until they clear — require Revenue Hub Enterprise.

Why this distinction matters for outbound teams specifically: most outbound-sourced deal desks don't need a general "stop the pipeline" gate — deal volume is too high and velocity is the point. What they actually want is the narrower one: auto-approve anything inside a standard discount band, and only flag the outliers. That's a Revenue Hub problem, not a Sales Hub Enterprise one, and buying the wrong tier to solve it means paying for a feature you don't need.

What a Fast, Outbound-Fit Quote Flow Looks Like

The version of this that actually keeps pace with an outbound pipeline's velocity looks like: line items pulled straight from the Product Library onto the deal record, a discount rule that auto-clears anything inside an approved band and only routes true exceptions to a manager, one-click quote generation from a template, e-signature sent in the same flow, and a payment link attached to the signed quote — all without a rep exporting a PDF, emailing it separately, and manually chasing a signature. Every extra manual step is a place a fast-moving, still-warm outbound prospect can go quiet.

When Native HubSpot Quotes Is Enough

For most SMB and mid-market deals sourced from cold email or LinkedIn outreach, native Quotes on Revenue Hub Professional covers the job: standardized pricing, one to three packaged tiers, a lower-to-mid ACV, and pricing that doesn't need to be reconfigured deal by deal. That's the profile of a high-volume outbound motion — speed to signature matters more than configurator sophistication.

When You Need Real CPQ Instead

The signal to look past native Quotes shows up when a deal needs any of: a guided-selling configurator for bundled or dependent products, an approval chain that has to cross more than one department (deal desk, legal, finance) rather than a single discount-threshold rule, or subscription mechanics — co-terming, mid-term proration, usage-based billing — that go beyond simple one-time or flat recurring payments. That's a smaller share of outbound-sourced deals, but it does show up as ACV climbs or as a deal moves upmarket.

Deal-Desk Friction Points Specific to Outbound Teams

  • Verbally-agreed pricing that never becomes a formal quote. A price discussed on a call but not captured in HubSpot creates renegotiation risk the moment a formal quote finally goes out.
  • Discount-approval bottlenecks on routine requests. Without a pre-approved discount band, a rep waits on a manager for a 10% discount that a logic-based rule could have cleared instantly — exactly the kind of delay a fast-moving outbound prospect notices.
  • Quotes that expire while a prospect goes quiet. Outbound-sourced buyers who go dark mid-quote force a resend-and-re-negotiate cycle that a slower enterprise deal desk rarely has to deal with at the same frequency.

On e-signature validity: HubSpot states its native e-signatures carry the same legal weight as a handwritten signature across the US, Canada, the EU, the UK, and other jurisdictions, and supports a buyer-plus-countersigner flow. HubSpot also explicitly advises consulting your own legal team on validity for your specific customers and contract types — treat that as a real instruction, not boilerplate, especially before relying on e-signature for regulated industries or cross-border deals.

A Lightweight Setup Checklist for Outbound Teams

  1. Decide if Revenue Hub Professional or Enterprise is the right tier. Professional covers standard approvals and basic e-signature volume; only move to Enterprise if you need logic-based, rule-driven approval routing.
  2. Build out the Product Library with your actual package tiers. Keep pricing standardized where you can — the more custom the pricing gets per deal, the less native Quotes alone will feel sufficient.
  3. Set one simple discount-approval rule before you build anything complex. Start with a single threshold (for example, auto-approve up to 15% off, route anything above it) rather than a full multi-tier rule set on day one.
  4. Build a clean quote template with your standard terms, payment options, and e-signature block already configured.
  5. Attach a payment link to the signed quote so signature and payment collection happen in the same motion instead of a separate invoicing step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does HubSpot have native CPQ?

Yes. HubSpot's CPQ (configure-price-quote) tools — Product Library, Quotes, quote approvals, and pricing rules — are built into Revenue Hub Professional and Enterprise, rather than sold as a separate standalone CPQ product.

What HubSpot tier do I need for the Quotes tool?

As of 2026, HubSpot Quotes requires a Revenue Hub Professional or Enterprise subscription, plus a Revenue Hub seat for each user who creates or edits quotes. It is no longer bundled into Sales Hub tiers the way it was under HubSpot's older packaging.

Can HubSpot Quotes handle recurring or subscription billing?

At a basic level, yes — Revenue Hub supports one-time and recurring payment collection directly from a quote. More complex subscription mechanics like co-terming, mid-term proration, and usage-based billing are handled more thoroughly by a dedicated CPQ platform such as DealHub.

What's the difference between HubSpot Quotes and full third-party CPQ tools?

Native HubSpot Quotes covers branded quote generation, e-signature, tiered pricing, and discount-approval rules. Dedicated CPQ tools like DealHub and PandaDoc add deeper guided-selling configurators, multi-department approval chains, and more advanced subscription and billing logic — capabilities most outbound-sourced deals don't need.

Are HubSpot's native e-signatures legally valid?

HubSpot states its e-signatures carry the same legal weight as handwritten signatures in the US, Canada, the EU, the UK, and many other jurisdictions, and supports buyer-plus-countersigner signing. HubSpot also explicitly advises consulting your own legal team to confirm validity for your specific customers and contract types.

Do outbound-sourced deals need real CPQ, or is HubSpot's native Quotes enough?

For most outbound-sourced SMB and mid-market deals — standardized pricing, lower-to-mid ACV, high deal volume — native HubSpot Quotes on Revenue Hub Professional is typically enough. Look at a dedicated CPQ platform only once you need multi-step product configuration, approval chains crossing multiple departments, or advanced subscription and usage-based billing.

The tier you need depends less on deal size than on how standardized your pricing already is.

By the time an outbound team is asking about custom objects, they usually already have data flowing into HubSpot — from a sync like the one we cover in syncing cold email and LinkedIn data into HubSpot, or from reply-driven workflows like the ones in workflow automation for reply handling. This post isn't about either of those layers. It's about the data-modeling decision that sits underneath both: once outbound activity is landing in HubSpot somehow, when does a piece of it deserve to be its own object, and when is a property on Contact, Company, or Deal enough?

The Short Answer

  • Custom objects need Enterprise on at least one Hub. That's a real cost floor to weigh before you design around them — Marketing Hub Enterprise alone lists at $3,600/month.
  • The test isn't "is this outbound-specific data" — it's "is this a many-to-many relationship." A campaign touching many contacts, and a contact touched by many campaigns, is the shape that justifies an object. A single attribute on one record doesn't.
  • Objects buy you native workflows and custom reports, not full standard-object parity. Know the specific gaps — no scoring, no funnel reports, no Sequences — before you commit a build to one.
  • Most first custom objects fail from over-modeling, not under-modeling. Teams build an object for something a property would have handled, then carry the setup and governance overhead for no relational benefit.

What a HubSpot Custom Object Actually Is

A custom object is a schema you define inside HubSpot's CRM: a name, a mandatory primary display property (the field that identifies each record at a glance — text, number, date, or dropdown, optionally required to be unique), any number of additional custom properties, and associations to standard objects (Contacts, Companies, Deals, Tickets) or to other custom objects. Once created, a custom object record behaves like a standard one — it has its own record page, its own properties, and it can be associated to and from anything else in your CRM.

What's Actually Gated Behind Enterprise

An Enterprise subscription is required to use custom objects at all, and separately required to manage custom object pipelines. The classic path is Marketing Hub, Sales Hub, or Service Hub at the Enterprise tier — once any one of those is active, custom objects are available across the whole portal, because they live in the shared CRM layer rather than inside one Hub's toolset. HubSpot's Hub lineup has continued to expand since custom objects launched, so check HubSpot's current pricing page for the full list of qualifying plans before you buy rather than assuming only the three original Hubs count.

Built in the UI, or Built via API

Most teams create a custom object through Settings > Data Management > Data Model, no code required: define the object, its primary display property, secondary display properties, and associations through a guided flow. Anything programmatic — bulk schema changes, objects built from a private app, or a data model managed in code alongside a broader integration — goes through HubSpot's CRM Objects Schema API instead. For most outbound RevOps builds, the UI path is enough; reach for the API only if the object needs to be created or modified as part of an automated deployment process.

Where Custom Objects Earn Their Keep for Outbound Teams

A Campaign or Sequence Object

One record per outbound campaign, associated to every contact enrolled in it. This is the case that most clearly justifies the many-to-many test: a single contact is realistically touched by multiple campaigns over their lifecycle, and a single campaign touches hundreds or thousands of contacts. Modeled as a property, you're left overwriting "current campaign" every time someone re-enrolls, losing the history of everything they were previously part of. Modeled as an object, campaign-level reporting — reply rate, meetings booked, by campaign — becomes a native report instead of a property-based approximation.

A Touchpoint or Engagement Event Object

One record per meaningful cross-channel action — an email open, a LinkedIn connection accepted, a reply — associated back to both the contact and the campaign. This is the pattern that lets multi-channel activity (email plus LinkedIn) show up as discrete, queryable history instead of a single "last touch" property that gets clobbered by whatever happened most recently.

Territory or Named-Account Data: Usually Not Worth a New Object

A "Territory" or "Named Account" custom object is a candidate pattern here too, but in practice most teams don't need it. We cover the mechanics of splitting accounts by region, industry, or size in territory and account assignment in HubSpot — a region/industry/size property on the Company record, combined with filtered workflows, covers this well without the Enterprise-tier cost of a dedicated object. Reach for a custom object here only if you need a territory to hold its own history (which reps have owned it, when it was last rebalanced) independent of whatever company happens to sit in it today.

Other Patterns Worth Knowing

The pattern generalizes past outbound: onboarding, orders, invoices, and subscription/renewal objects are common post-close use cases documented across RevOps agency writeups, evidence that the same many-to-many test applies well beyond the top of funnel.

What Custom Objects Can't Do

The gap between "custom object" and "standard object" is specific, not total:

  • No lead scoring. The Score property is scoped to contacts, companies, and deals — it isn't available on custom objects.
  • No funnel reports on custom object pipelines. Even though a custom object can have its own pipeline with stages, funnel reporting stays limited to contact, lead, company, and deal objects.
  • No HubSpot Sequences support. Sequence personalization tokens only read from contact and company properties. To use data that lives on a custom object inside a sequence email, you generally have to copy the relevant value onto the contact record first via a workflow.
  • Owner properties and activities aren't pre-built. Standard objects come with an owner property and activity associations (calls, emails, tasks, notes) configured automatically. On a custom object, you add a HubSpot-user-type property to serve as owner and set up activity associations yourself — skipping this is one of the most common first-build mistakes.

Numbers we refuse to repeat: specific figures for how many custom object schemas, properties per object, or records per object HubSpot allows circulate across agency blogs (commonly "10 objects," "500 properties," "500,000 records"), but HubSpot's own documentation declines to publish a universal number and instead points admins to their portal's Data Model screen or Products & Services Catalog. The exact ceiling varies by subscription. Don't build around a specific number without checking your own portal first.

Common Mistakes on a First Custom Object

  1. Building before mapping the data model on paper. A quick entity-relationship sketch — what associates to what, one-to-many or many-to-many — catches most design mistakes before they're expensive to unwind.
  2. Modeling everything as an object when a property would do. The over-engineering trap: an object built for a single-value attribute carries setup and governance overhead for zero relational benefit.
  3. Sloppy primary display property choices. If the display property isn't the thing a human would actually use to identify the record at a glance, every list and association picker built on top of the object becomes harder to use.
  4. Forgetting association labels and direction. An association without a clear label ("Campaign → Contact: Enrolled In" vs. just an unnamed link) turns into a guessing game for anyone who didn't build it.
  5. Assuming feature parity with standard objects. Scoring, funnel reports, and Sequences not working on custom objects surprises teams mid-build, not during planning — know the gaps going in.

A Quick Gut-Check Before You Build One

Four questions worth answering honestly before committing to a custom object:

  • Is this genuinely a many-to-many relationship, or am I just trying to avoid overwriting a property?
  • Do I need native HubSpot reporting and workflow automation against this entity, or would an external tool serve just as well?
  • Am I already on Enterprise for other reasons, or would this be the sole justification for the upgrade?
  • Is the expected data volume something HubSpot's own object limits can comfortably hold, or does this belong in a system built for high-volume event data instead?

If the honest answer to the first question is "not really," a property or an external sync almost always beats the object — the Enterprise cost and setup overhead only pay for themselves once the relationship is genuinely relational.

Frequently Asked Questions

What HubSpot plan do I need to use custom objects?

You need an Enterprise-tier subscription on at least one HubSpot Hub — typically Marketing Hub, Sales Hub, or Service Hub Enterprise. Once any one qualifying Enterprise subscription is active, custom objects become available portal-wide, since they live in the shared CRM layer rather than inside a single Hub's toolset. HubSpot's Hub lineup has expanded since custom objects launched, so check HubSpot's current pricing page for the up-to-date list of qualifying plans.

Can a custom object have its own pipeline, like a Deal?

Yes. Custom objects support their own pipelines with custom stages, separate from the Deal pipeline — useful for a stage-based process, like onboarding or renewal, that isn't the sales pipeline itself. Managing custom object pipelines also requires an Enterprise subscription. Funnel reports, however, aren't available for custom object pipelines the way they are for Deals.

Should I use a custom object or a custom property for outbound campaign data?

Use a property when the data is a single fact about one contact or deal, such as "current sequence step." Use a custom object when the data is its own entity with a many-to-many relationship to your standard objects — one contact touched by many campaigns over time, or one campaign touching many contacts — where you need to report on or automate against the entity itself, not just tag it onto a contact record.

Can custom objects be used in HubSpot workflows and reports?

Yes — custom object records can trigger and be acted on by workflows, and can be built into custom reports and dashboards, the same general way contacts, companies, and deals can. Two notable exceptions: custom objects can't use the lead-scoring Score property, and they can't be used in HubSpot Sequences, since sequence personalization only reads from contact and company properties.

How many custom objects can I create in HubSpot?

HubSpot does cap the number of custom object schemas, properties per object, and records per object, but the exact ceiling varies by subscription and isn't published as one universal number in its help documentation. Check your portal's Data Model screen or your Products & Services Catalog for your specific limits rather than assuming unlimited, especially if you're considering a high-volume object like per-touch engagement events.

What's an example of a custom object an outbound sales team might build?

A common pattern is a "Campaign" or "Sequence" object — one record per outbound campaign, associated to every contact enrolled in it — paired with a "Touchpoint" or "Engagement Event" object recording each meaningful cross-channel action and associated back to both the contact and the campaign. This supports campaign-level reporting and multi-touch history without overwriting single-value properties on the contact every time they're re-enrolled or touched again.

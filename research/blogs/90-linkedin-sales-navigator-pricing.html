TL;DR

  • Every tier gets the same 50 monthly InMail credits and the same 50+ search filters. The $40/month jump from Core to Advanced doesn't buy you more InMails or better search — it buys team features.
  • Native CRM sync to HubSpot or Salesforce is Advanced Plus only. If bidirectional sync into your CRM is the reason you're buying Sales Navigator, Core and Advanced won't get you there.
  • Annual billing saves 25% on Core but only about 6% on Advanced — an inconsistency worth checking against your own quote before assuming the same discount applies at every tier.
  • Sales Navigator is the sourcing layer, not the outreach engine. It finds and tracks the right people; it doesn't send your sequences or manage your pipeline — that's still a cold email tool and a CRM's job.

The Short Answer

  • Core costs $119.99/month per seat ($1,079.88/year, a 25% annual discount). It's a single-user plan with no team seat management.
  • Advanced costs $159.99/month per seat ($1,799.88/year, roughly a 6% annual discount). Up to five seats can be bought self-serve, without a sales call.
  • Advanced Plus is quote-only and is the only tier with native bidirectional CRM sync to HubSpot, Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics 365, or Oracle Sales.
  • Solo sellers rarely need more than Core. The InMail count and search filters are identical to Advanced — the extra spend buys collaboration features a one-person operation doesn't use.

Sales Navigator Core: Who It's Actually For

Core is a single-user plan — there's no seat management, no shared lists, and no team visibility. What it does include, at the lowest price point, is the full 50+ filter search set and the same 50 monthly InMail credits as every other tier. For a founder or solo rep running their own outbound alongside cold email prospecting, that's usually enough — Advanced's team features (TeamLink, shared lists, usage reporting) have nothing to coordinate when there's only one person using the seat.

Sales Navigator Advanced: The Team Tier

Advanced adds Smart Links, TeamLink warm-introduction visibility, Buyer Intent signals, CSV book-of-business upload, and usage reporting — and it's the point where team seat management shows up, with up to five seats purchasable self-serve before a sales conversation becomes necessary. The InMail allowance and search filters don't change from Core; the value case for Advanced is entirely about coordination across more than one rep and visibility into what the team is doing, not more raw prospecting capacity per seat.

Sales Navigator Advanced Plus: The CRM-Native Tier

Advanced Plus is the only tier with bidirectional CRM sync — LinkedIn names HubSpot, Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales, and Oracle Sales explicitly as supported integrations, with lead/contact auto-save, CRM-side filtering and badges, activity writeback, and full ROI reporting. It also adds SSO and Employee Data Integration for enterprise provisioning, TeamLink Extend, and a Microsoft Teams embed. Pricing is not published — LinkedIn quotes it based on team size, CRM integration scope, and onboarding needs, which means the only way to get a real number is a sales conversation. Third-party reports put street pricing somewhere in the neighborhood of $1,600+/seat/year (directional — not confirmed on LinkedIn's own page, and likely to vary by deal size); treat any number you see quoted elsewhere as a starting point for negotiation, not a list price.

Free Trial and the Annual vs. Monthly Math

Core and Advanced both offer a free trial that requires a credit card and can be cancelled anytime. LinkedIn's own trial-terms page doesn't commit to a fixed length — it states trial length "may vary based on region or promotional terms" — but third-party reports consistently describe 30 days as the standard length, with a 60-day trial sometimes available through an existing subscriber's referral link (directional). Advanced Plus, being sales-managed, isn't offered as a self-serve trial.

The annual discount is worth checking carefully rather than assumed: Core's annual plan saves 25% off the monthly rate, but Advanced's annual plan only saves about 6% — a meaningfully smaller discount than Core gets, per LinkedIn's own current pricing page. If you're comparing annual commitments across tiers, don't assume the same percentage discount applies to both; run the actual numbers.

Sales Navigator vs. Recruiter vs. Premium Business

These three LinkedIn products get confused often enough to warrant a quick disambiguation. Premium Business (from $69.99/mo) and Premium All-in-One (from $89.99/mo) are general networking and personal-branding subscriptions, capped at 15 and 30 InMails per month respectively, with none of Sales Navigator's 50+ B2B search filters, Buyer Intent, TeamLink, or CRM integrations. LinkedIn Recruiter is built for finding candidates to hire, not buyers to sell to. Sales Navigator carries the highest InMail allowance of any LinkedIn product (50/month, at every tier) and is the only one of the three purpose-built for prospecting.

Where Sales Navigator Fits in a Real Outbound Stack

Sales Navigator is the sourcing and signal layer — finding the right people, tracking Buyer Intent, and building targeted lists — not the full outbound motion by itself. It doesn't send your outreach sequences and it doesn't manage your pipeline once a prospect replies. In practice, it sits upstream of a dedicated outreach tool (whether that's LinkedIn's own InMail and connection requests, or a paired cold email cadence) and feeds into a CRM like HubSpot for pipeline tracking and deal management. Sales Navigator's own sanctioned data-movement paths — InMail, Smart Links, CSV export, and Advanced Plus's native CRM sync — don't carry that risk. Which Tier Should You Actually Buy

A simple, directional checklist — not LinkedIn's official guidance, since LinkedIn's own materials don't publish fit-by-team-size recommendations beyond generic tier descriptions:

  • One person doing their own outbound: Core. You get the same filters and InMails as every other tier at the lowest price.
  • A small outbound team that needs shared visibility: Advanced, once coordination (shared lists, TeamLink, usage reporting) starts to matter more than raw seat cost.
  • A team where CRM sync and ROI attribution are a hard requirement: Advanced Plus — but go in with a clear scope of what you actually need synced, since pricing is negotiated per deal.
  • A team under roughly 10–15 seats considering Advanced Plus mainly for the CRM sync: price out a manual or middleware-based sync into HubSpot first — the custom-quote overhead of Advanced Plus often isn't worth it below that scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does LinkedIn Sales Navigator cost in 2026?

Sales Navigator Core is $119.99/month (or $1,079.88/year, a 25% annual discount) and Advanced is $159.99/month (or $1,799.88/year, about a 6% annual discount), per seat, per LinkedIn's official pricing page. Advanced Plus is quote-only, priced by team size and CRM needs.

What's the difference between Sales Navigator Core and Advanced?

Core and Advanced share the same 50+ search filters, 50 monthly InMail credits, and 10,000-lead save limit. Advanced adds team features Core lacks: Smart Links, TeamLink warm introductions, Buyer Intent signals, shared team lists, usage reporting, and self-serve seat management for up to five licenses.

Does Sales Navigator sync with HubSpot?

Native, bidirectional CRM sync with HubSpot — along with Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics 365, and Oracle Sales — is available only on Sales Navigator Advanced Plus, LinkedIn's top-tier, custom-priced plan. Core and Advanced don't include CRM sync.

Is there a free trial for LinkedIn Sales Navigator?

Yes — Core and Advanced both offer a free trial that requires a credit card and can be cancelled anytime, commonly reported as 30 days, though LinkedIn's own terms note trial length can vary by region and promotion. Advanced Plus, being sales-managed, isn't offered as a self-serve trial.

What is Sales Navigator Advanced Plus, and who needs it?

Advanced Plus is LinkedIn's enterprise tier, built for sales teams that need Sales Navigator data embedded directly in their CRM. It adds bidirectional CRM sync, full ROI reporting, SSO/admin controls, and TeamLink Extend on top of everything in Advanced. Pricing is custom and requires a sales conversation.

How is Sales Navigator different from LinkedIn Recruiter or Premium Business?

Premium Business is a general networking and personal-branding subscription with limited search and only 15 InMails a month — it isn't built for prospecting workflows. LinkedIn Recruiter is a hiring tool for finding candidates. Sales Navigator is purpose-built for B2B sellers, with the highest InMail allowance of any LinkedIn product (50/month) and 50+ filters designed for prospecting rather than hiring or networking.

The right tier depends on whether you're buying search capacity or CRM integration — and those two things are priced very differently.

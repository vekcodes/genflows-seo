The Recipes

1. Claygent Navigator: scrape sites that block scrapers

Navigator (launched late 2025) is a browser-using agent — it fills search forms, clicks filters, and extracts from JavaScript-heavy or gated directories that defeat normal scraping. Setup: input URL column → Navigator column with explicit interaction steps ("search for {keyword}, apply the {industry} filter, extract the results table as JSON") → split the JSON into columns. At 3 credits per row, prototype on 10 rows in Sandbox Mode first. Best targets: association member directories, conference attendee lists, competitor pricing pages.

2. Competitor G2 reviewer poaching

Claygent scrapes a competitor's G2 reviews → filter to 1–2 star ratings → a second Claygent finds the reviewer's LinkedIn and company → email waterfall → AI column writes an opener referencing the specific pain in the review. This is the highest-relevance cold angle that exists: the prospect has publicly documented the problem you solve. Keep the send cohorts small and the copy restrained — reference the frustration category, never quote the review verbatim.

3. The free jobs-data signal engine

Clay indexes roughly 18 million LinkedIn job posts, queryable at zero data-credit cost — the most underrated free signal on the platform. Source: job postings matching pain keywords (e.g., "hiring SDRs," "RevOps manager") → dedupe by company domain → enrich decision-makers → free AI formula qualifies ("does this JD imply in-house outbound? Y/N + evidence"). A company hiring three SDRs is telling you its pipeline strategy; this pairs directly with our signal-based prospecting playbook.

4. Website visitor → warm outbound loop

Clay added native RB2B and Vector integrations in April 2026. Pipe visitor identification into a Clay table via webhook → enrich and verify → formula-score on fit (size, title match) → route: high scores go to a Smartlead/Instantly sequence plus a Slack alert; group rows by domain so two visitors from one account escalate priority. Guardrails: enable hot-leads-only at the source and add dedupe rules before the webhook, or repeat visitors will flood the table. More on intent routing in our intent data guide.

5. Job-change champion tracking

Import closed-won contacts and past champions → Signals hub → "Monitor job changes" → on trigger: waterfall the new work email, Claygent researches the new company's fit, Slack alert with an auto-drafted note referencing the prior relationship, write-back to CRM. Champions who move are your warmest possible outbound — reply rates on these campaigns routinely run several times cold baseline (cold average is just 3.43% in 2026 per Instantly's benchmark; see our benchmarks breakdown).

6. Self-healing CRM enrichment loop (Growth tier)

HubSpot Lookup as a source on an auto-update schedule → carry the HubSpot Record ID on every row → enrich, score, dedupe → Update action keyed on record ID with "Ignore blank values" ON — the single setting that prevents Clay from overwriting populated CRM fields with nulls. Run nightly. This turns data decay (B2B data rots ~2–3% a month) into a solved background process.

7. BYO-API-key credit arbitrage (Growth tier)

HTTP API columns calling Prospeo, FullEnrich, or LeadMagic directly with your own API keys, chained with conditional run settings ("only run if previous column is empty") — you rebuild the waterfall at direct-API prices instead of Clay's marketplace rates. Sculptor now auto-configures the endpoint, auth headers, and request body from a plain-English description, which removes most of the technical barrier this tactic used to have. Run the math for your volume: at tens of thousands of enrichments a month, the savings pay for the Growth tier by themselves.

8. Multi-table qualification pipeline

Never run expensive enrichment on unqualified rows. Table 1: TAM companies (CPJ search or Lookalikes seeded from your customer list) → Claygent Neon qualification (2 credits) → send only qualified rows to Table 2 for contact finding and waterfalls → Table 3 for copy → sequencer push. Clay's Functions feature (March 2026) lets you define the scoring logic once and reuse it across every table. Qualify-then-enrich typically cuts total credit burn 40–60% versus enrich-everything.

9. Signal-stacked scoring with free AI formulas

AI formula columns cost zero credits — use them for all conditional logic. Stack three or more signals per account (funding, hiring velocity from workflow #3, technographics, web intent, LinkedIn activity) → weighted score via formula → tier into cohorts of ≤50 for segmented sends (small cohorts reply 2.76× better than 1,000+ blasts) → route tiers to different campaigns, with email non-responders flowing to a HeyReach LinkedIn campaign. The scoring model itself is covered in our lead-scoring guide.

10. The deep personalization column stack

One research column per angle (recent LinkedIn posts, news, job descriptions, case-study match) → one AI snippet column per angle with a SKIP fallback prompt pattern: "Write one sentence referencing {signal}. If the signal is vague or missing, return exactly: SKIP." → a free formula column picks the best non-SKIP snippet → push as custom variables to your sequencer. The SKIP pattern is what separates usable AI personalization from the generic slop prospects now delete on sight — depth of personalization lifts replies 32–142% depending on how specific the signal is. Total cost: ~4–5 credits and ~6 actions per row, roughly $40–60 per 1,000 fully personalized leads at Growth-tier rates.

Guardrails That Save Real Money

  • Sandbox first. Sculptor Sandbox Mode (rolled out to all users June 2026) validates workflows without burning credits. Ten test rows before every full run, no exceptions.
  • Conditional runs everywhere. Every enrichment column should have a run condition — waterfalls that fire unconditionally are the #1 cause of surprise credit burn. A 3-step waterfall on 1,000 leads can consume 3,000–9,000 credits.
  • Model selection is a cost decision. Claygent Helium (1 credit) for simple lookups, Neon (2) for qualification, Argon/Navigator (3) only for genuine multi-step research. Defaulting everything to Argon triples your bill.
  • Watch the plan gates. HTTP API and CRM auto-sync require Growth — budget for that before designing workflows 6 and 7.

Frequently Asked Questions

What changed in Clay's 2026 pricing?

In March 2026 Clay moved to a dual system: Data Credits (marketplace data purchases) plus Actions (platform operations like AI columns, HTTP requests, and exports). Launch runs ~$167/mo annual with 3,000 data credits + 15,000 actions; Growth ~$446/mo with 6,000 + 40,000. Unused data credits roll over up to 2× your monthly amount. Full analysis in our Clay pricing review.

What is Claygent Navigator?

A browser-using AI agent inside Clay that interacts with websites the way a human does — filling forms, clicking filters, paginating — so it can extract data from JavaScript-heavy or gated sites that block conventional scrapers. It costs 3 credits per row, so reserve it for sources you genuinely can't get any other way.

Which Clay plan do advanced workflows need?

Most of the ten run on Launch. Two are gated to Growth: anything using HTTP API columns (workflow 7) and native CRM auto-sync (workflow 6). The free plan's 200-row table limit makes it a testing ground only.

Do these workflows work with Smartlead or Instantly?

Yes — Clay pushes to Smartlead, Instantly, HeyReach, Woodpecker, and most major sequencers natively, mapping enriched columns to custom variables. Tool trade-offs are in our cold email software comparison.

Is AI personalization at scale still effective in 2026?

Only with quality control. Average reply rates fell to 3.43% in 2026 largely because of undifferentiated AI outreach — but specific, signal-grounded personalization still roughly doubles replies versus generic copy. The SKIP-fallback pattern in workflow 10 is the difference: send nothing rather than send filler.

TL;DR

  • Almost nobody publishes a price. We opened 33 RevOps agency and HubSpot partner domains. Eight published a number we could actually read.
  • Published retainers cluster $4,500–$27,000/month; fixed-fee implementations run $1,500–$12,580. Roundup ranges quoting "$25,000+ enterprise" sit outside every verified card.
  • The price is not the problem — the denominator is. One vendor publishes hours (so its $185/hr is checkable), one publishes a ceiling on hours, one reserves 20% of what you buy before work starts, and one publishes monthly tiers with no hours at all.
  • Your HubSpot partner earns 20% of your subscription MRR for up to three years — HubSpot's own partner rules. The person advising you between Professional and Enterprise has an annuity riding on the answer.
  • A Solutions Partner can waive HubSpot's required onboarding fee ("up to $6,000 value per client"). A Solutions Provider cannot. Ask which one you are talking to.
  • Programmable automation is gated to Data Hub Professional. Sales Hub Enterprise at $150/seat/month does not include it — this is the most common mid-project upgrade.
  • HubSpot's own paid migration refuses to move workflows and reports at every price tier. That single exclusion is the best-sourced argument for why a RevOps engagement exists at all.

Buying RevOps help is the hardest purchase in the revenue stack to price-check, and not because agencies are unusually shady. It is because RevOps is the only outbound-adjacent service with no natural billing unit. We have written the same teardown for cold email agencies and LinkedIn lead gen agencies; in both cases the market had settled on a unit you could count. RevOps has not.

Sourcing note: every price below was read on the operator's own live page on 2 September 2026 and is labelled as such. Figures from roundups, salary aggregators, or vendors estimating someone else's market are marked (directional) in-text. Prices move — re-confirm before you sign.

The Short Answer

For a HubSpot implementation, expect $1,500–$12,580 as a one-time fixed fee, driven almost entirely by hour count rather than by scope description. For ongoing RevOps work, published monthly retainers cluster $4,500–$19,000, with capped-hours "pod" models running to $27,000. Fractional RevOps sits at $2,500–$15,000/month for 8–60 hours. Across every verified card, the implied hourly rate lands in a surprisingly tight band: roughly $185–$310 per hour.

That band is the category's only useful benchmark — and it only applies if the proposal says how many hours you are buying. Most do not.

Why RevOps Has No Billing Unit

Cold email is priced by volume: sends, mailboxes, domains. You can count all three, so you can compute cost per meeting and argue about it. LinkedIn is priced by seats, because every major tool meters per connected account — so a LinkedIn retainer is seats multiplied by time, and the cost curve is near-linear.

RevOps is priced by hours you cannot see. There is no send volume, no seat count, no deliverable you can tally at month end — a CRM architecture either holds up or it does not. So the market sells either time (hourly, capped-hours retainers, points) or an undefined "project," and because neither is externally countable, the price carries no information on its own.

One practical consequence should reorganise how you read every proposal: you cannot price-shop RevOps, because there is nothing to compare. You can only make the deliverable falsifiable. Scope definition, not negotiation, is your leverage.

The Denominator Test

Stop reading the price and start reading the denominator, and the category reorganises itself. One question, applied to every card: how many hours does this figure buy, and who decides?

ModelDenominator published?Implied rateWhat can go wrong

Capped-hours retainer (RevPartners) Partly — "max hours/week" ~$208–$227/hr at the cap (our arithmetic) A ceiling is not a guarantee. Bill 60% of the cap and your real rate is ~$350/hr

Flat monthly partnership (Denamico) No Not computable You cannot benchmark the rate or detect under-delivery. Requires outcome-based terms to be safe

The denominator also exposes that volume discounts in RevOps are close to fictional. Kiwi's points are $75 flat at every tier — buying 3.3× more earns a 0% discount — and RevPartners' implied rate falls only from about $227/hr to $208/hr across its range, roughly 8% for tripling your spend. If a vendor says the big tier is better value, ask them to show it per hour.

One question that reprices any proposal. "How many hours is this, and what happens to hours we don't use?" HubSpot's own recurring consulting contracts state that unused hours "expire at the end of each month and will not carry over," and that fees are "non-cancellable" and "non-refundable." If your vendor's answer is vaguer than its own vendor's, that is your answer.

How Your HubSpot Partner Gets Paid Twice

This disclosure appears in no RevOps pricing article we found, yet it is published openly on HubSpot's own partner pages.

Solutions Partners earn 20% of your monthly recurring subscription revenue, for up to three years (Solutions Providers get 12 months). Partners also pay HubSpot for programme membership, "starting from $400/month." So the consultant advising you whether you need Professional or Enterprise has a three-year annuity attached to the answer, and a cost base that rewards keeping you subscribed.

To be fair: this is normal channel economics, it is disclosed, and it does not make the advice wrong. It does mean the tier recommendation is the one line where you should want a second opinion, and where "you'll want Enterprise" deserves the question which specific gated feature are we buying?

The same relationship contains a genuine, underused buyer win. HubSpot's partner sales rules state that "a benefit of being a solutions partner is the ability to waive product onboarding for your clients if you are providing those services," quantified elsewhere on HubSpot's site as "up to $6,000 value per client." Critically, the same page states that solutions providers cannot waive onboarding. Two consequences:

  • Ask which one you are hiring. Partner and Provider are different HubSpot statuses with different powers over your invoice.
  • Do not pay both. If you are buying Professional or Enterprise and hiring an implementation partner, the onboarding fee is waivable — you sign a confirmation electing partner services instead. Paying HubSpot $1,500 and an agency $3,000 for the same stand-up is a common, avoidable double-charge.

Worth noting alongside that: one partner's Fast Track implementation prices exactly match HubSpot's required onboarding fees — $1,500 for Sales Hub, $3,000 for Marketing Hub. That is our synthesis of two published pages, not a vendor claim, but read next to the waiver rule it looks deliberate: same money, execution instead of guidance. HubSpot's own onboarding page supports the point, noting that if "you need additional help executing, you'll want to enlist a certified Solutions Partner."

The Software Bill Underneath the Retainer

An agency retainer sits on top of your HubSpot bill, and the RevOps-relevant part is not what most articles cover. Start with a naming correction that invalidates a lot of competing content: Operations Hub no longer exists. It is now Data Hub — hubspot.com/pricing/operations redirects to /pricing/data, and HubSpot's legal catalogue lists no Operations Hub at all. ("Commerce Seat" is likewise now "Revenue Seat.") Any 2026 article quoting "Operations Hub pricing" is quoting a dead SKU.

Data Hub editionPriceSeats / creditsOnboarding fee
Starter $7/mo per seat annually ($20 monthly) Per seat; 500 credits None
Professional $720/mo annually ($800 monthly) 1 Core Seat included, +$50/seat; 5,000 credits None
Enterprise $2,000/mo, annual billing only 1 Core Seat included, +$75/seat; 10,000 credits None

Contradiction worth knowing: HubSpot's legal catalogue lists Data Hub Professional at $800/month; its pricing page lists $720 annually. The catalogue appears to publish monthly rates only, so HubSpot's own "list price" differs by roughly 10% depending which HubSpot page you read, and neither is labelled authoritative.

Two things in that table do real work. Data Hub carries no mandatory onboarding fee, so a RevOps-first purchase avoids the onboarding tax entirely while a Sales-Hub-first purchase pays $1,500–$3,500 before an agency invoices anything. (Our HubSpot Sales Hub pricing breakdown covers the seat tiers and that fee in full — and this research settles an open question it flagged: the $7,000 figure in older content is real, but it is Marketing Hub Enterprise, not Sales Hub.)

Second, the upgrade that ambushes projects mid-build: programmable automation — custom workflow actions written in code — is gated to Data Hub Professional and appears in no other hub's feature table. Not Sales Hub Enterprise, not Smart CRM Enterprise, not Revenue Hub Enterprise. You can spend $150 per seat per month and still be unable to run a coded workflow action. Webhooks, data quality automation, duplicate management, scheduled triggers and calculated properties sit behind the same gate. Enterprise-only, separately: custom objects, sandboxes, field-level permissions, data warehouse integrations and saved permission sets.

Two line items to settle before signing, not after.

Credits. Each Breeze action executed in a workflow costs 10 credits, and credits reset monthly without rolling over. One Breeze action across 5,000 records a month burns 50,000 credits — ten times the Data Hub Professional allotment — roughly $450/month at $0.010 per overage credit on those assumptions. HubSpot's stated default is that if usage scales beyond your limit "your account will automatically be upgraded," and that upgrade holds "for the remainder of your commitment term." Ask your agency to model credits for anything it automates.

Your agency's seat is free. The Partner Seat is "available at no additional cost" (granted at HubSpot's discretion), and View-Only Seats are free and unlimited on Starter, Professional and Enterprise. Any agency quoting you a seat cost for its own access is wrong.

What HubSpot Won't Migrate — and Why the Retainer Exists

HubSpot sells its own paid CRM data migration in three tiers, and the tiering tells you what it believes drives scope: record count and custom object count. Bronze covers 350,000 records and zero custom objects; Silver 1,000,000 and up to two; Gold under 5,000,000 and up to four. All three carry a 60-day delivery period.

Now the exclusion, identical at every tier and quoted from HubSpot's own contract: "Migrating workflows and reports are not included in scope."

That is the whole argument for a RevOps engagement, written by the vendor. HubSpot moves your data and explicitly not your process. Rebuilding automation and reporting — the actual substance of RevOps work — is structurally never vendor scope; it is always agency or in-house scope. Also excluded at every tier: flat-file data, and for Salesforce sources, quotes, quote line items, pricebooks, enhanced notes, attachments outside the standard file cabinet and campaign assets. HubSpot warns separately that "data in formula fields may not transfer as expected."

So when a proposal says "migration," make it say which half. And put the automation and reporting rebuild on the scope list as named deliverables — your deal stages, routing rules, lead scoring, lifecycle stages and dashboards are all rebuild work by definition, not migration work.

The Only Hour Benchmarks Anyone Publishes

HubSpot publishes no hours guidance for its own Hub onboarding — only delivery windows (60 days for Sales Hub Professional, 90 for Sales Hub Enterprise, 120–150 for Customer Platform tiers). It publishes no price for any consulting or migration service either; the four mandatory onboarding fees are the only service prices it lists. Everything else requires a sales conversation.

But it does publish hours on its consulting add-ons, and those are the only per-unit effort anchors that exist anywhere in the category:

HubSpot consulting serviceHoursWindow
Data Hub programmable automation — launch of one workflow-based process using coded actions and webhooks 6 hours 90 days
Data Hub data sync — one connected system 6 hours 90 days
Technical consulting: standard / advanced / premium 8 / 12 / 16 hours 60 / 90 / 120 days
Migration consulting 4 hours 90 days
Private training (live) 8 hours, +2 per added block —

Use these arithmetically. An agency quoting 40 hours for "workflow automation" is proposing roughly six processes by HubSpot's own yardstick — so ask which six, and hold any integration estimate against the 6-hours-per-connection anchor. On timeline, HubSpot's only published end-to-end figure is on its Salesforce switch page: "We typically see most businesses complete their migration process within three months," hedged as depending on your own speed. Its developer team separately estimates a 1–3 week design phase for a moderately sized source system.

What nobody publishes: effort per unit of scope beyond those two 6-hour anchors, and any duration for adoption — which HubSpot names as one of its five migration phases and gives no timeline at all. Every implementation estimate you receive, ours included, is practitioner judgement layered on those two numbers.

Agency, Fractional, or Hire?

Our RevOps manager job description and salary benchmarks already covers the salary bands, why sources disagree, and the fractional-versus-hire timing question. What it does not do is apply the denominator, so that is all this section adds: hours purchased per dollar.

A US RevOps Manager base of roughly $124,557 (Salary.com, 1 September 2026; directional), loaded at the 1.43× multiplier implied by the Bureau of Labor Statistics' March 2026 employer cost data ($46.60 total hourly compensation against $32.60 in wages, private industry; our arithmetic), is about $178,000 a year — and roughly 2,080 hours. The same money buys about $14,800/month of fractional capacity, the top of every published tier, or roughly 720 hours a year. So the framing is not "agency is cheaper": the hire buys about 3× the hours, the agency buys bench depth, seniority on day one and no ramp. One person cannot be architect, developer, analyst and trainer at once; a pod can. Which constraint binds you differs by stage — our RevOps maturity model and minimum viable RevOps stack answer that better than any price card. Two cautions: every published fractional-versus-hire comparison we found was written by a firm selling fractional, and the "1.25× to 1.4× salary" loading rule repeated across HR content has no traceable origin and sits below what BLS measures.

The Numbers in Every Pitch That Nobody Can Source

Two statistics dominate this purchase decision. We chased both to their origins and are publishing neither as fact.

"30–70% of CRM implementations fail." Every citation chain we followed dead-ended in a blog citing a blog. Gartner, the firm most often credited, has no public statement of a CRM failure rate at all; its only matching asset is a paywalled document numbered in the early 2000s. The frequently-quoted 63% "Merkle Group study" is dated 2013 by some sources and 2016 by others, with no report title or sample size anywhere — a statistic that cannot agree on its own publication year is not a statistic. The one figure with an identifiable owner, a consultancy's 55%, is that firm's own survey with no disclosed sample size. If a proposal opens with a CRM failure rate, ask for the report. Nobody has ever sent us one.

The salary spread on "HubSpot Administrator." Aggregators put it at $36,000 (ZipRecruiter, 25th percentile), $80,783 (Glassdoor) and $110,831–$118,881 by experience (Salary.com) — a 3.3× range on one title. Two failure modes are visible: one aggregator appears to index part-time and virtual-assistant listings, and a widely-cited $54,836 figure is for an administrator employed by HubSpot the company, not a HubSpot admin at yours. Any article quoting one HubSpot Administrator salary is quoting noise.

Every row below is a tier gate or exclusion published by HubSpot, which makes them the cheapest pre-signature checks available. A proposal promising anything in the right column on the wrong plan is wrong on its face, not negotiable.

That last row is the one people skip. HubSpot's Solutions Partner Program Agreement is reassuring on ownership: the end user "will own and retain all rights to the End User Data," retains portal access "regardless of whether you placed the order," and partners cannot independently hold your subscription. The most transparent agency terms we read confirm IP in deliverables "shall be owned by Customer," while reserving reuse of the firm's own "proprietary templates, workflow, and processes." Neither mentions who holds super-admin, or what you get on the way out — ask for both in writing, the same discipline we recommend for CRM data hygiene ownership and outbound data in HubSpot.

One term to diary: the most transparent card in the category also auto-renews "for additional terms of equal length…in perpetuity," with 21 days' written notice to stop it on a six-month initial term. A points-based vendor at the other end offers a 30-day opt-out. That gap is worth more than a few hundred dollars of monthly rate.

So What Should You Actually Pay?

Grounded in the verified band, as ranges because that is what the evidence supports:

  • Single-hub implementation, Professional tier: $1,500–$4,000 for a fast, templated stand-up; $7,000–$13,000 for a genuinely configured one with 40–70 hours behind it. Get the hour count, then divide.
  • Ongoing RevOps, small team: $5,000–$8,500/month buys 20–25 hours of a senior operator. Below about $2,500/month you are buying calendar access, not work.
  • Ongoing RevOps, pod model: $9,850–$19,000/month for 10–20 hours a week of a multi-skill team. Verify whether the hours are a floor or a ceiling.
  • Benchmark rate: $185–$310/hour is the verified band. Above $400/hour for build work needs a reason, and any quote you cannot convert into an hourly rate needs a denominator before it needs a negotiation.
  • Add the software floor: roughly $14,000–$16,000 a year for a six-person team on Sales Hub Professional plus Data Hub Professional, billed annually, including the waivable onboarding fee. Custom objects or a data warehouse connection push you to Enterprise and change that materially.

The one-line version: in cold email you audit the volume, in LinkedIn you count the seats, and in RevOps the only thing you can audit is the scope you wrote down before you signed. Write it down.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a RevOps agency cost per month in 2026?

Published monthly retainers from vendors that disclose pricing cluster between $4,500 and $19,000, with capped-hours pod models running up to $27,000. Fractional RevOps runs $2,500 to $15,000 or more per month for 8 to 60 hours of work. Across every verified card, the implied hourly rate falls in a fairly tight $185 to $310 band, which is the only benchmark worth applying. Be aware that only 8 of the 33 operator sites we opened published a number at all, and that roundup articles quoting $25,000-plus "enterprise" retainers name no vendors and cite no sources.

How much does a HubSpot implementation cost?

Verified published prices span $1,500 to $12,580 for what is described as broadly the same job. One partner sells 14-to-30-day implementations from $1,500 for Sales Hub, $3,000 for Marketing Hub and $4,000 for a bundle. Another sells hour-capped onboarding packages at a stated $185 per hour: 15 hours for $2,775 up to 68 hours for $12,580, over 4 to 12 weeks. The spread is driven by hour count, not by scope description, so always ask how many hours the fee buys before comparing two quotes.

Can I avoid HubSpot's onboarding fee if I hire an agency?

Usually yes, and this is the most commonly missed saving. HubSpot requires onboarding on Professional and Enterprise: $1,500 for Sales or Service Professional, $3,000 for Marketing Professional, $3,500 for Sales or Service Enterprise and $7,000 for Marketing Enterprise. But HubSpot's partner rules state that a Solutions Partner can waive product onboarding for clients when the partner is providing those services, described elsewhere on HubSpot's site as up to $6,000 of value per client. You sign a confirmation electing the partner's services instead. Critically, a Solutions Provider cannot waive it, only a Solutions Partner can, so ask which status your vendor holds. Note also that Data Hub carries no mandatory onboarding fee at all.

Which HubSpot tier do I need for custom-coded workflows?

Data Hub Professional, at $720 per month billed annually. Programmable automation, which is HubSpot's term for custom workflow actions written in code, appears in no other hub's feature table. Sales Hub Enterprise at $150 per seat per month does not include it, and neither does Smart CRM Enterprise or Revenue Hub Enterprise. Webhooks, data quality automation, duplicate management, scheduled workflow triggers and calculated properties sit behind the same Data Hub Professional gate. This is the most common mid-project upgrade: the agency needs a coded action and the buyer discovers a new monthly line item. Note also that Operations Hub has been renamed Data Hub, so any article quoting Operations Hub pricing is quoting a retired product name.

Does HubSpot's paid migration service move my workflows and reports?

No. HubSpot's CRM Data Migration service carries the same exclusion at all three price tiers: migrating workflows and reports is not in scope. Flat-file data is also excluded, and for Salesforce sources so are quotes, quote line items, pricebooks, enhanced notes, attachments outside the standard file cabinet and campaign assets. HubSpot also warns that data in formula fields may not transfer as expected. This means rebuilding your automation and reporting is structurally never the vendor's job, and is always either agency or in-house scope. It is the clearest published argument for why a RevOps engagement exists.

Is it cheaper to hire a RevOps person than to pay an agency?

It depends on whether you need hours or bench depth, and the honest answer is that the hire buys more hours while the agency buys more skills. A US RevOps Manager base of about $124,557, loaded at the 1.43 times multiplier implied by the Bureau of Labor Statistics' March 2026 employer cost data, comes to roughly $178,000 a year plus recruiting, with a ramp that vendor sources put at 3 to 6 months. That is about 2,080 hours. The same money buys roughly $14,800 a month of fractional capacity, which at published tiers is about 60 hours a month, or 720 hours a year. So the hire buys roughly three times the hours, while an agency or fractional operator gives you seniority from day one, several skill sets at once and no ramp. Treat every published fractional-versus-hire comparison with care, because all of the ones we found were written by firms selling fractional services.

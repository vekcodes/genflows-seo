Close is built for teams that sell by sending and calling, and its automation is the part that makes that scale. Most outbound teams either under-use it or set it up in a way that keeps emailing people who already replied.

The Short Answer

Use Close Workflows for the parts of outbound that mix email with calls and tasks, and always attach a goal so a reply stops the run. Keep volume cold-email-safe by sending from several mailboxes, because Close adds no sending allowance beyond your mailbox provider. If you need LinkedIn steps, run them in a LinkedIn tool and sync the activity in rather than waiting for a native step.

What a Close Workflow Can Do

Close's help center describes a Workflow as an automated series of steps run against leads or contacts. Older pages and URLs call the same thing a sequence, so you will see both words in guides and in the docs address. The settings below are the ones that decide whether it works for cold outbound.

Setting What Close's docs say Why it matters for outbound
Step types Email, SMS, call, task creation and assignment, lead updates, opportunity creation One flow can mix email touches with call tasks
Delays 1 minute up to 365 days, in minutes, hours or days Tight early follow-ups, long nurture gaps
Send window Default Mon–Fri, random time 9AM–4PM in the contact's timezone; customisable Looks less robotic than a fixed send time
Blackout dates Block automated email and SMS steps only Calls and tasks still fire, so check them
Enrollment Manual, bulk from list views, or automatic by trigger Triggers can come from form submissions and CRM events
Run mode Run Once is the default for workflows with communication steps; Run Multiple is explicit Stops accidental re-sends to the same contact
Recipient If empty, the lead's Primary Contact is used Set the recipient deliberately on multi-contact leads

Goals: Stop Emailing People Who Replied

The most important setting is the goal. Close's docs list six goal types, and a run pauses when its goal is met. Without one, a workflow keeps going after a prospect has replied, which is the fastest way to annoy a warm lead.

Goal Pauses the run when Use it for
Incoming email The contact replies by email Every cold email workflow
Incoming SMS or call The contact texts or calls back Multi-channel workflows
Meeting booked A meeting is booked Stops chasers after a booking
Lead status change A rep changes the lead status Manual qualification, not-interested
Outcome met A custom outcome is reached Opportunity created, demo held

For cold email, set the goal to incoming email at minimum, and add meeting booked if you book calls through a scheduling link. Most communication goals need a recipient contact on the lead, so check that every enrolled lead has one. See our follow-up cadence guide for the timing to build into the steps.

Sending Limits and Mailboxes

Close's docs say it sets no sending cap of its own. Email goes through your connected SMTP, Gmail or Office 365 account, so your provider's limits apply. Close also warns that sending more than 1,000 emails in 24 hours may make your email server halt outbound mail, and suggests a bulk email service for high volume. Email steps follow your overall sending limits, and other email activity can delay them.

Each user must also turn on "Allow automated Workflows to send on your behalf" in Settings, and the default email account is set under Settings, then Email. In practice, that means cold outbound should be split across several warmed mailboxes rather than one connected account. Reporting and What to Measure

Close's workflow reporting shows runs completed, percentage of goals met, average time to goal, bounce and error rates, and response by step. Open rates are left out on purpose.

Open rates are deliberately left out of Close's workflow reporting, because of Apple Mail Privacy Protection. Judge a workflow on replies, goals met and bounces instead. We cover why in our Apple Mail Privacy Protection guide.

Workflow reporting is not available on Close's legacy Base plan, per its help center.

Where Workflows Stop

  • LinkedIn: Close's Workflows page does not document a LinkedIn step. Run LinkedIn in a dedicated tool and sync it in, as we describe in the HeyReach Close CRM integration guide.
  • Archived leads: archiving a lead pauses active runs, and on reactivation they fast-forward without the delay, so review them before reactivating.
  • Mass triggers: Close can pause runs through rate limiting when too many triggers fire at once.
  • Routing: replies still need an owner and a response-time rule. See our lead routing and speed-to-lead guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Close Sequences and Workflows the same thing?

Close's current help center documents one automation feature called Workflows, with email, SMS, call and task steps. The older docs URL still says "sequences" and returns the same content, so many guides use the two words interchangeably. Close's page does not explicitly say one replaced the other, so we use "Workflows" and note the older name.

Which Close plans include Workflows?

Close's docs say Workflows are only available on the Growth and Scale plans. Solo and Essentials do not include them, and its pricing page lists bulk email and the power dialer on the same two plans.

What happens when a prospect replies?

If you set a goal such as incoming email, incoming SMS, incoming call, meeting booked or lead status change, the run pauses when it is met. Close then counts that contact as converted in reporting. Without a goal, the run keeps going, so set one on every outbound workflow.

Can I enroll leads in bulk?

Yes. Close's docs describe bulk enrollment from the Leads or Contacts list views using the Workflow icon, as well as manual enrollment on a single record and automatic enrollment from triggers. Bulk emailing itself needs Growth or higher.

Does Close limit how many emails I can send?

Close does not set its own cap. Emails go through your connected mailbox, so your provider's limits apply, and Close warns that sending more than 1,000 emails in 24 hours may cause your email server to halt outbound mail. For cold volume, spread sending across several mailboxes.

When do automated messages go out?

By default on weekdays, at a random time between 9AM and 4PM in the contact's timezone. You can change the window and add blackout dates, which block only automated email and SMS steps.

What a RevOps Manager Actually Does

Revenue Operations unifies the systems, data, and processes behind sales, marketing, and customer success — one owner for the revenue engine instead of three teams with three conflicting spreadsheets (the full case is in our Sales Ops vs RevOps comparison). At manager level, that translates into five recurring responsibility blocks, which we pulled from real, live 2026 postings rather than template articles:

  • Own the CRM and GTM systems. Admin-level Salesforce and/or HubSpot ownership — workflows, custom objects, data hygiene — plus the surrounding stack (Gong, Apollo, LeanData, Clay in multiple current postings). See our RevOps tech stack guide for the six zones this covers.
  • Run forecasting and pipeline cadence. Design the forecast, run pipeline reviews, own territory design and lead routing. One current posting: "partner with GTM leaders as the dedicated RevOps owner to drive forecasting, pipeline, capacity" (Brex, $133K–$166K).
  • Build the reporting layer. "Dashboards and reports that give leadership clear visibility into pipeline health, conversion rates, and revenue performance" — the 12 metrics that matter are in our RevOps dashboard guide.
  • Automate the process itself. The 2026 shift: postings now say things like "design, automate, and continuously improve the processes that run sales, marketing, and customer success, with AI as a core part of your engineering workflow" and require "shipped production automations (n8n, Zapier, Make, or Workato)" (Vim, $130K–$160K).
  • Advise revenue leadership. Cross-functional alignment, handoff SLAs, comp and quota administration, deal desk — and at good companies, a literal seat next to the CRO ("serve as advisor to the CRO" — Embrace, ~$150K).

The 2026 Skill List (What Changed)

The classic core hasn't moved: admin-level Salesforce or HubSpot, forecasting, BI dashboards (Looker/Tableau/Power BI), lead routing, data hygiene, and the cross-functional diplomacy to make three departments follow one process. What's new is that AI ate the configuration work, and postings now price the difference — practitioners with AI-systems fluency command materially more (one 2026 analysis pegs "AI Ops" specialists near $200K vs ~$140K for generalists; treat the exact gap as directional, the direction as real). Concretely, 2026 reqs now ask for:

  • SQL you wrote yourself plus data-warehouse familiarity (BigQuery appears in current postings).
  • Shipped production automations — n8n, Make, Zapier, or Workato, named explicitly.
  • Modern GTM tooling: Clay showed up in three of the five real JDs we pulled; Gong and Apollo are near-universal; one posting lists "Claude/Anthropic prompt design and API-based workflows" as a plus.
  • Demonstrated AI use in the last 1–2 years with concrete examples — verbatim requirement language, and a fair interview question in both directions.

Certifications, honestly ranked: Salesforce Admin ($200, 40–80 hours) is the most-requested credential in enterprise postings and the best paid-ROI move; HubSpot's Revenue Operations certification (free, ~10–15 hours) is the best free curriculum. Claims that certifications add 10–18% to offers circulate without solid methodology — treat certs as interview access, not automatic pay.

RevOps Manager vs GTM Engineer: The Boundary Nobody Draws

Several 2026 "RevOps Manager" postings — running Clay enrichment, HeyReach LinkedIn campaigns, email deliverability, n8n pipelines — are GTM Engineer jobs wearing a RevOps title. The working boundary: RevOps owns the system of record and the revenue process (CRM, forecasting, routing, reporting, comp); GTM engineering owns the growth machinery (enrichment pipelines, outbound automation, signal engines). Small companies rightly combine them in one hire; past ~$10M ARR they diverge, because process governance and pipeline-building are different full-time jobs. If your req mixes both, decide which half is the day job before you interview — candidates optimized for one half will underwhelm at the other.

Hiring Guidance: When, Who Reports Where, and the Fractional Math

  • When to hire #1 RevOps: the composite trigger across 2026 hiring guides is $4–5M ARR or 10–15 reps — or qualitatively, when your CEO/VP Sales spends 10+ hours a week on CRM admin, when sales/marketing/finance report different revenue numbers, or when forecast accuracy drops below ~80%. Earlier-stage? Our RevOps for startups guide covers what to run before the first hire.
  • Reporting line by stage: under ~$50M ARR (often no CRO yet) → CEO or senior-most commercial leader; $50M–$500M → CRO (the most common answer); $500M+ → dedicated RevOps org under CRO or COO. CFO-reporting exists as a neutrality play but is the minority; systems-heavy roles occasionally report to a CIO.
  • The full-time true cost: base +30–40% for benefits and overhead, a 25–35% search fee if you use recruiters ($40–50K on a senior role), and a 30–90 day ramp before high-quality decisions. A "$140K hire" is a ~$190K+ Year-1 commitment.
  • Fractional/agency alternative: senior fractional RevOps runs $4K–$7K/mo for 15–25 hrs/week at the $2–10M ARR stage ($3K–$15K/mo across the wider market; agency engagements $5K–$20K/mo). Below ~$3M ARR, fractional is almost always the right first move; between $3M–$25M it often beats both consulting and a full-time hire on cost per outcome when scoped to function ownership. Decision framework parallels our agency vs in-house guide.
  • First 90 days to expect: 0–30 audit (funnel, stack, data quality, stakeholder interviews) → 31–60 quick wins (standardized stages, routing fixes, forecast cadence) plus a roadmap → 61–90 single source of truth, weekly GTM dashboard, handoff SLAs. Our maturity model is a ready-made audit rubric.
  • Interview questions that separate operators from admins: "Describe integrating sales, marketing, and CS data into one dashboard." "How do you reconcile discrepancies between forecast and actuals?" "Walk me through a CRM optimization you led — what broke, and how did you get adoption?" "What would your first 90 days here look like?" (the prioritization tell), plus a live probe of CAC/LTV/sales-velocity fluency and — new for 2026 — "show me an automation or AI workflow you shipped."

Market Context: Is RevOps Still Growing?

Yes — with a correction to a stat you'll see everywhere. "Head of Revenue Operations" topped LinkedIn's Jobs on the Rise list in 2023 (many articles still cite it as current); the 2026 growth signals are steadier: RevOps director postings growing ~15% YoY, roughly 170K+ open RevOps-related US roles per ZipRecruiter-derived data, and adoption at 48% of companies overall (84% of enterprises) — up from a third in 2020. Gartner's much-quoted prediction — 75% of the highest-growth companies deploying a RevOps model — was for 2025, and directionally arrived. The 2026 twist is the role's centre of gravity: as AI agents take over pipeline hygiene and forecasting mechanics, the human job shifts from configuring systems to governing them — deciding what the agents automate, what data they trust, and where humans stay in the loop. That's also why the skill list above looks the way it does.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a RevOps Manager earn in 2026?

US average total compensation is ~$125K–$130K (Glassdoor: $128,660; typical band $102K–$164K), with posted big-metro ranges clustering $125K–$170K mid-level and $150K–$210K senior. Add ~10–20% bonus and equity as standard. ZipRecruiter's lower ~$96.5K average reflects all industries and markets — benchmark against your segment.

What's the difference between a RevOps Manager and a Sales Ops Manager?

Sales Ops serves the sales team; RevOps owns the full revenue engine across sales, marketing, and customer success — one process, one data model, one forecast. The full comparison (and which your team needs first) is in our Sales Ops vs RevOps guide.

When should a startup hire its first RevOps person?

Around $4–5M ARR or 10–15 sales reps — earlier if leadership is drowning in CRM admin or teams report conflicting revenue numbers. Below ~$3M ARR, fractional RevOps at $4K–$7K/mo typically beats a full-time hire, whose true Year-1 cost runs ~35–40% above base salary.

What skills should I test for in a 2026 RevOps hire?

The classic core (Salesforce/HubSpot admin, forecasting, dashboards) plus the new bar: SQL they wrote themselves, a production automation they shipped (n8n/Make/Zapier), and concrete AI workflows from the past year. Ask for artifacts, not claims — a dashboard walkthrough and an automation demo reveal more than any behavioral question.

How do people get into RevOps?

The most common feeder is Sales Operations (~20% of RevOps professionals came from it), followed by marketing ops, CS ops, FP&A, and consulting. The ladder runs Analyst → Manager → Senior Manager/Director → VP, with compensation roughly doubling over the first decade — and increasingly, RevOps leadership is a credible path to CRO (Notion's CRO came up through RevOps at GitHub).

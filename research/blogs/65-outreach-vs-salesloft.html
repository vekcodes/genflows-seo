The Short Answer

Outreach and Salesloft are both sales engagement platforms (SEPs) — sequencing, dialer, conversation intelligence, and reporting built for reps and managers running structured outbound and follow-up motions. Outreach leans toward larger, Salesforce-anchored revenue orgs that want deep automation and AI-driven deal/call intelligence. Salesloft has historically won on ease of setup and rep experience, and as of December 2025 it's merged with Clari, adding a real forecasting layer that Salesloft didn't previously have on its own. Neither is HubSpot-native in the way a HubSpot-first RevOps team would want, and neither is a substitute for a cold email sending tool like Smartlead or Instantly — they solve a different stage of the pipeline.

What Changed: The Clari-Salesloft Merger

If the last thing you read about Salesloft described it as "a Vista Equity Partners company," that's now out of date. Vista Equity Partners took a majority stake in Salesloft in a deal announced in December 2021 at a $2.3 billion valuation, after Salesloft passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue. That much is unchanged history.

What's new: Salesloft completed a merger with Clari, the forecasting and pipeline-intelligence platform, on December 3, 2025. The combined company pitches itself as a "Predictive Revenue System" — sales engagement plus forecasting under one roof, something Salesloft couldn't offer on its own before. (Directional) Third-party synthesis puts the combined entity at 5,000+ customers and roughly $450 million in combined ARR, with the first joint product release — Clari's forecasting layered into Salesloft's execution side — shipping in April 2026. As of the most recent reporting available, integration is still in progress: the two products run separate interfaces rather than one unified platform, and pricing for new bundled deals has reportedly moved upward.

Why this matters for your decision: if you're evaluating Salesloft specifically because you need better forecasting, you're now evaluating a company mid-merger, not a finished product. Ask any vendor rep directly what's actually shipped and unified today versus what's still roadmap.

Pricing: Nobody Has Verified Numbers

Both platforms are quote-only. Neither Outreach nor Salesloft publishes a public pricing page with per-seat rates — every number you'll see in a blog post, including this one, is a third-party estimate, not a vendor disclosure.

To make that concrete: pricing-intelligence sites disagree with each other by roughly 2x. One source estimates Outreach at around $3,000 per user per year and Salesloft at around $1,000 per user per year; others estimate both in the $100–$185 per user per month range across their respective tiers (Outreach's "Amplify" Core/Plus/Pro; Salesloft's Essentials/Advanced/Premier), with implementation fees reportedly running $5,000–$25,000+ on top for Outreach, and Salesloft's dialer possibly sold as a separate add-on rather than bundled. (All directional — no primary source publishes these figures, and the estimates contradict each other.)

The honest takeaway: don't budget off any number you read online for either tool, including the ones above. Get a quote from both, and negotiate — enterprise software at this price tier rarely closes at list.

Where HubSpot Fits (and Doesn't)

This is the part most comparison content glosses over: neither Outreach nor Salesloft is HubSpot-native. Both were built with Salesforce as the primary CRM design target, and it shows in the integration depth.

  • Outreach: Salesforce is the deepest, most mature integration. HubSpot and Microsoft Dynamics are supported as native connectors, but positioned as secondary to the Salesforce relationship.
  • Salesloft: ships a native HubSpot "CRM Sync" connector — contacts and accounts sync bidirectionally in near real time — but deal/opportunity data only flows one way (HubSpot → Salesloft), activity data only flows the other way (Salesloft → HubSpot), and cadence/sequence-step data doesn't sync natively at all. Custom HubSpot objects need API or third-party tooling to bridge. The connector also requires HubSpot Sales Hub Pro or Enterprise. (Directional — consistent pattern across integrator sources, not a single vendor doc.)

HubSpot's own comparison content (yes, HubSpot publishes one) recommends Outreach for high-volume, complex multi-touch teams and Salesloft for mid-market teams prioritizing coaching visibility — then, unsurprisingly, steers readers without a dedicated RevOps function toward HubSpot's own Sales Hub instead. That's a fair pitch to be skeptical of coming from HubSpot, but the underlying integration gaps it points to are real and worth confirming directly with either vendor before you buy. If your team is fully committed to HubSpot as the system of record and doesn't have the RevOps bandwidth to manage a bolted-on integration with known sync gaps, native HubSpot Sequences may cover more of your actual need than either SEP, even though its automation depth doesn't match a dedicated platform.

Where Outreach and Salesloft Fit in the Outbound Stack

The most common mistake we see teams make when comparing these tools: treating them as alternatives to a cold email sending tool like Smartlead or Instantly. They're not — they solve different problems, and they sit at different tiers of the outbound stack.

Layer Job Example tools
Cold email infrastructure High-volume sending, inbox rotation, deliverability/warm-up, at low per-seat cost Smartlead, Instantly
Sales engagement Multi-channel sequencing, dialer, conversation intelligence for reps working live prospects Outreach, Salesloft
CRM / revenue system of record Pipeline, deal stages, forecasting, reporting across the full funnel HubSpot, Salesforce

A team using Smartlead or Instantly to generate cold pipeline doesn't automatically "graduate" to Outreach or Salesloft — that upgrade only makes sense once reps are managing real volume of multi-touch, multi-channel follow-up on live replies and booked meetings, and need dialer plus conversation intelligence layered on top. Buying a $100+/seat/month sales engagement platform to do what a cold email tool already does — raw high-volume sending — is a mismatch regardless of which SEP you pick.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Outreach or Salesloft better for HubSpot users?

Neither is HubSpot-native — both were built with Salesforce as the primary CRM design target. Salesloft's HubSpot connector syncs contacts and accounts bidirectionally but only syncs deal/opportunity data one-way and doesn't sync cadence-step data natively; Outreach treats Salesforce as its deepest integration and HubSpot as secondary. Teams fully committed to HubSpot as their system of record often get more out of native HubSpot Sequences than bolting on either platform.

Do Outreach and Salesloft publish their pricing?

No. Both are quote-only, custom-priced per seat with annual contracts. Third-party estimates circulating online range widely — some sources cite roughly $1,000–$3,000 per user per year, others $1,200–$2,200 — and disagree with each other by roughly 2x, so treat any specific number you see, including the ones in this post, as unverified until you get a quote directly.

Can I use Outreach or Salesloft instead of a cold email tool like Smartlead or Instantly?

Not really — they solve different problems. Smartlead and Instantly are built for high-volume cold email sending and inbox deliverability at low cost. Outreach and Salesloft are sales engagement platforms built for reps managing engaged prospects through multi-channel sequences, calls, and forecasting. They sit a tier above cold email infrastructure in the outbound stack, not in competition with it.

What happened to Salesloft after the Vista Equity Partners deal?

Vista Equity Partners took a majority stake in Salesloft in a deal announced in December 2021 at a $2.3 billion valuation. More recently, Salesloft merged with forecasting platform Clari, completing in December 2025, to combine sales engagement with revenue forecasting under what the companies call a "Predictive Revenue System." As of mid-2026 the two products were still being integrated, with separate interfaces and pricing that has reportedly moved up for new bundled deals.

Which tool has better AI features, Outreach or Salesloft?

Outreach's Kaia handles AI conversation intelligence — call transcription, sentiment analysis, and AI-generated meeting summaries. Salesloft's AI layer, Rhythm, prioritizes seller actions based on buyer signals (including from its Drift chat product), and since the Clari merger is being extended with forecasting AI. They overlap but aren't identical: Outreach leans toward call and deal intelligence, Salesloft leans toward signal-based action prioritization and, increasingly, forecasting.

Should a small or growing sales team choose Outreach or Salesloft?

Usually neither is the first tool a small team should buy — both require real budget and onboarding investment, and reviewers consistently describe a learning curve (steeper for Outreach, milder but present for Salesloft). Teams without a dedicated RevOps function, or those still building outbound volume, are typically better served starting with a cold email tool for pipeline generation and HubSpot for engagement, then evaluating a sales engagement platform once rep call/sequence volume actually justifies the cost.

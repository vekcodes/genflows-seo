TL;DR

  • Three plans: Email from $55/mo, Multichannel from $87 per user per month (both on yearly billing, 20% off) and Enterprise on custom pricing. Monthly billing lists at $69 and $109.
  • The pricing unit differs by plan. Email has unlimited users and is priced by monthly email volume. Multichannel is per user, with 5 senders each, and is the plan that adds LinkedIn, SMS and calling.
  • Credits are the hidden meter. Multichannel includes 400 credits per user per month and Email includes none. An email lookup costs 5 credits and a phone lookup 20, so 400 credits is about 80 emails or 20 phone numbers.
  • No refunds on unused fees. The Terms say lemlist will not refund unused fees for a month or year already begun, and plans auto-renew. The 14-day trial is your only free test.
  • Per-seat pricing gets expensive for teams. On our arithmetic, 10 Multichannel users cost $870 a month on yearly billing before mailboxes or extra credits.

Lemlist is one of the few cold outreach tools that sells email, LinkedIn, SMS and calling in one product, which makes its price harder to compare than a pure sending tool. The plan fee depends on whether you pay per user or per email volume, lead data runs on a separate credit meter, and mailboxes, domains and some channels sit on top. This guide lays out every plan, the credit rules, the extras that cost money, the trial and refund terms, and a worked monthly total for teams of one, three and ten.

The Short Answer

A solo user who wants email plus LinkedIn lands on Multichannel at $87 a month on yearly billing, and a team that only sends email can pay $55 a month regardless of headcount. Once you add mailboxes, a one-person Multichannel setup with 5 mailboxes costs roughly $137 a month and a 10-user team with 50 mailboxes roughly $1,370, before domains and extra credits. Email suits email-only teams of any size, Multichannel suits reps who also work LinkedIn and calls, and Enterprise suits teams that need SSO, custom roles and a dedicated account manager. Budget separately for lead data, because credits drive the real spend.

What separates the tiers

  • Email to Multichannel is a change of pricing unit as well as features. Email gives you unlimited users and senders for a volume-based price. Multichannel adds LinkedIn automation, SMS and a call dialer, but charges per user and caps you at 5 senders each.
  • Multichannel to Enterprise adds the ability to run lemlist from your CRM, custom roles and permissions, a dedicated account manager, custom terms, SSO and SAML, and priority support.
  • Shared across plans: lemlist lists an email and phone finder, a 650M+ lead database, AI agents, warmup and CRM integrations on the plan cards. Its pricing FAQ says lemwarm and deliverability protection are included in all plans, though the free trial excludes lemwarm.
  • Team rule: lemlist's help center says all members of a team must be on the same plan, so you cannot put only your LinkedIn-heavy reps on Multichannel.

Check SMS and WhatsApp before you count on them. The Multichannel plan card lists SMS automation as a feature and WhatsApp as an add-on, but the page's FAQ groups SMS and WhatsApp together as add-ons. We did not find a price for either, so we are not stating one.

Credits: The Meter Behind Lead Data

Lemlist's database and enrichment run on credits, charged per successful action. The pricing page describes credits as pay per success with unlimited rollover, while the help center adds important detail on which credits roll over.

What is included

  • Free trial: 200 credits, one time.
  • Email plan: no included credits.
  • Multichannel: 400 credits per user per month. These reset monthly and do not roll over.
  • Enterprise: 400 per user per month for teams created from 18 December 2025. Older Enterprise teams keep 1,500 or 2,500, and lemlist says the allowance may be reduced or removed in a future update.

The practical effect: a Multichannel user's 400 included credits cover about 80 email lookups or 20 phone numbers a month, on our arithmetic. A rep who sources their own lists will exhaust that quickly, and one credit pool is shared by the whole team, so any member can drain it. Only Enterprise has per-user credit caps.

Mailboxes and Domains

You can connect your own Google or Microsoft mailboxes, or buy them inside lemlist. According to lemlist's help center, mailboxes bought in the app start at $10 a month each for Google or Outlook and $5 a month for SMTP. A domain must be bought with at least one mailbox, is locked for one year and renews annually unless you turn off auto-renew. Bought domains and mailboxes are non-refundable, and an unpaid mailbox subscription is deleted by the provider along with its emails. We did not find a published domain price, so our totals below leave domains out.

Free Trial, Refunds and Renewal

  • Free trial: 14 days, no credit card. The pricing page says it gives access to Multichannel features except in-app calls, plus 200 credits. Lemlist's help center describes a 50-emails-a-day cap and no lemwarm during the trial. Some promotional links show a 30-day trial banner instead; we saw one while reading the pricing page, so the standard offer is 14 days.
  • After the trial: the account moves to a free tier rather than billing you automatically.
  • Auto-renewal: paid subscriptions renew on the same monthly or annual basis.
  • Terminating: you can terminate a paid plan at any time, but the Terms say lemlist will not refund unused fees for a month or year already begun, even pro rata.
  • Credits: unused credits that expire are lost without refund. Mailboxes and domains bought in lemlist are non-refundable.

The lesson is the same as for any tool with no refund window: prove deliverability and reply handling inside the trial, and avoid paying annually until a campaign has produced meetings. Compare that with the refund windows in our Smartlead pricing guide.

How Lemlist Compares on Price Structure

Lemlist's Email plan, at $55 for a 50,000-email tier with unlimited users, sits close to Instantly's and Smartlead's mid plans on sending volume, and all three advertise unlimited sending accounts or senders on their email plans. The real difference is Multichannel. Instead of pairing a sending tool with a separate LinkedIn tool, lemlist charges per user for both. Whether that is cheaper depends on team size: a solo user on $87 beats a $97 sending plan plus a separate LinkedIn seat, but a 10-person team on $870 a month is a different calculation. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does lemlist cost in 2026?

The Email plan starts at $55 a month on yearly billing ($69 monthly), and Multichannel is $87 per user per month on yearly billing ($109 monthly). Enterprise is custom. Credits, mailboxes, domains and WhatsApp cost extra.

Is lemlist priced per user?

Multichannel is priced per user, with 5 senders each. The Email plan has unlimited users and senders and is priced by monthly email volume instead, so a large email-only team does not pay per seat.

Does lemlist have a free trial?

Yes. The standard trial is 14 days with no credit card, with 200 credits and access to Multichannel features except in-app calls. Some promotional links show a 30-day trial. When it ends, the account drops to a free tier.

Are lead credits included in lemlist plans?

Multichannel includes 400 credits per user per month, and the Email plan includes none. An email lookup costs 5 credits and a phone lookup 20. More credits start at $16 a month for 2,000 on yearly billing, or $28 as a one-off top-up.

Does lemlist offer refunds?

No refunds on unused fees. The Terms say lemlist will not refund unused fees for a month or year already begun, even pro rata, and subscriptions auto-renew. You can terminate at any time, but the paid period still runs. Domains and mailboxes bought in lemlist are non-refundable.

What extra costs should I budget for with lemlist?

Mailboxes bought in lemlist start at $10 a month for Google or Outlook and $5 for SMTP, and domains renew annually. Heavy phone or signal use drains credits quickly, WhatsApp is an add-on, and scaling LinkedIn may need more accounts because of LinkedIn's daily limits.
